# Turn the search score into a calibrated likelihood

**Yes, doppler_print can match better, and most of the gain comes from swapping three heuristics in `dp-core::search` for their exact counterparts, not from an exotic engine.** The three heuristics are these. (1) The clipped, whitened log-power z-sum in `score.rs` should become a power-domain log-likelihood ratio, with a harmonic-presence mixture and a constrained Viterbi band. (2) The per-cell "best single hypothesis minus 3 z per unexplained flight" in `pipeline.rs` and `hypothesis.rs` should become one association likelihood. Belief propagation (BP) sums that likelihood over event-to-flight assignments, and it carries clutter, unknown aircraft and silence. (3) Rank-based `keep_top` refinement with a hand-set `DEFAULT_TEMPERATURE = 3.0` should become a mass-driven quadtree, which yields mode masses and regions calibrated against coverage. Geometry supplies cheap seeds and a clock-free coarse score. A per-event CPA (time, slant) pair is exactly linear in (x, y, δ) once the mirror side is chosen. In a numpy prototype that closed form located the receiver to **8–20 m median with 3–8 events**. An Equal-Differential-Time (EDT) score removes the clock entirely and survived one wrongly associated event at **21–59 m, where a δ-profiled L2 score missed by 1.4–2.4 km**. The recommended order is measurement first. Instrument `eval` and run five measurements that settle the open disagreements between notes. Then adopt, in order: the power LLR with an overlap correction; calibrated output; BP association; nuisance marginalisation; the tube and harmonic mixture; seeding, EDT and VarPro-LM; retrieval and branch-and-bound; and semi-coherent warping last. **No number in this report has been validated on dp-core or the corpus.** Each is either literature-sourced, a **[derived]** algebraic result, or a **[SIM-proto]** result from a standalone numpy prototype. Literature APIs (OpenAlex, arXiv, Semantic Scholar) rate-limited the researchers with HTTP 429, so several sources below were confirmed only by title or metadata, and they are marked as such.

## Today's search maximises a z-sum and leaves four losses on the table

Today's code is simpler than `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` describes, and the recommendations have to start from the code. For each cell, `pipeline::score_cell` enumerates association hypotheses depth-first in `hypothesis::associate`. The enumeration is capped at `max_hypotheses = 64` and requires per-event implied clocks to agree within `clock_tol_s = 8`. Each hypothesis is scored with `score::score_joint`, and the cell keeps the **single best** one, scored as Σ_e z_e − 3·(unexplained flights within 3 km) ([pipeline.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/search/pipeline.rs), [hypothesis.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/search/hypothesis.rs)). Each event's z is a weighted sum of the scoring plane along the predicted log-frequency curve, divided by √(n_frames·Σw²). The plane is a single 1 s Hann window at a 0.25 s hop, whitened log power, max-filtered ±1 bin with a 1 z/bin penalty and clipped to ±8. Per event, z is maximised over an f0 slide in 0.25-bin steps and over a clock sweep of ±5 s in 0.25 s frames around the association's mean clock ([score.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/search/score.rs)). Three items the notes treat as existing do not exist in the code yet. The f0 slide is a **direct loop**, not the FFT correlation the architecture doc describes. **No Nelder–Mead polish** exists: refinement only splits the top 200 cells down to 25 m. **No time-shifted-ADS-B null** is implemented. `likelihood.rs` is now committed (1219785) and maps scores to exp((s − s_max)/T) with T = 3 z. Its `hpd_level` assumes equal-area samples ([likelihood.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/search/likelihood.rs)). `eval` reports error, flight-tuple rank by peak score, z margin and clock error. It reports no coverage, no log score and no stage at which the truth was lost ([eval/mod.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/eval/mod.rs)).

Against that baseline the notes identify four losses with clear remedies. The first is statistical efficiency at low SNR. Under noise, normalised STFT power is Exp(1), and the sum of m such powers has a χ²₂ₘ null ([Ransom et al. 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)). A tone of per-bin SNR s makes the exact per-pixel LLR −s + ln I₀(2√(sP)), which is s(P − 1) at weak SNR. A 400k-draw Monte Carlo of weak-signal deflection **[derived]** prices today's transforms against linear power: taking the log costs **−1.3 dB**, max-pooling 4 bins in log costs **−4 dB**, and a ±2-bin max filter costs **−4.8 dB** ([signal_level_matching.md §4](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/signal_level_matching.md)). Those pooling figures assume independent bins, so they overstate the loss at low frequency. At 96 bins per octave a log bin is 0.0072·f wide, while a 1 s Hann window resolves about 1 Hz. The max-pool therefore combines about 0.7 independent cells at 100 Hz, about 2 at 300 Hz and about 7 at 1 kHz **[derived, this report]**. The loss is negligible for GA fundamentals and approaches the Monte Carlo figure only for jet tones and high harmonics. The second loss is association. Taking the best single hypothesis instead of summing hypotheses means one mis-associated flight can carry a cell. The 64-hypothesis depth-first cap can also silently drop the true tuple in busy airspace **[derived, this report]**. The third is the scale problem. Summing per-event z-scores gives each event equal weight whatever its length or SNR, and the 3 z silence penalty has no likelihood meaning. The fourth is the output. Cells are ranked by peak, never by integrated mass, and T is set by fiat.

## One target architecture, in five layers

The notes converge on a single pipeline when it is read as one probabilistic model. Discrete labels (event-to-flight assignment, harmonic index, clutter) sit on top. Per-event linear nuisances (f0, drift) come next, then a few shared nonlinear nuisances (δ, c̄, w̄, clock rate ε), and finally the 2-D receiver position. Each layer is handled in the cheapest way that is exact enough. This is the INLA pattern: Laplace for the latent part and numerical integration over a few hyperparameters, deterministic and without MCMC mixing problems ([Rue et al.](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01248v1)). It is also the BAYESTAR pattern. BAYESTAR marginalises nuisances with "very low order Gaussian quadrature", marginalises phase analytically to a Bessel-I₀ term, samples the sky on an adaptive HEALPix grid, and produces maps "comparable in accuracy to the full analysis" in seconds rather than hours ([Singer & Price 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.03634)). The table maps each layer to modules. Every new file stays under 300 lines. All computation stays in `dp-core`. Nothing touches `sim`.

### Closed-form loci and clock-free voting replace blind cell enumeration

A constant-velocity pass is a two-parameter shape (M = v/c, T = d/v), plus a time shift and a frequency scale, and all four can be read from a fitted event in closed form. The asymptote ratio r gives M = (r − 1)/(r + 1). f(t_c) = f₀ exactly. The CPA slope identity df/dt|_{t_c} = −f₀M/T was checked numerically to four decimals **[derived]** ([fast_retrieval.md §1](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/fast_retrieval.md)). When ADS-B supplies the trajectory, an event's CPA arrival time and slant range give two equations that are **exactly linear in (x, y, δ)** for a chosen mirror sign on a straight, level segment in still air. One is the lateral offset n̂·(r − A) = ±√(d² − H²). The other is the timing û·r/v − δ = t − d/c + û·A/v. Stacking events and enumerating sign patterns gives a 3×3 weighted least-squares solve per pattern. With σ_t = 0.1 s and 2% slant error, the prototype gave **46 m median (2% of trials over 500 m) with 2 events, 20 m with 3, 12 m with 5 and 8 m with 8** **[SIM-proto]** ([search_optimisation.md §1](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/search_optimisation.md)). The sign patterns whose χ² falls within a threshold of the best *are* the mirror and along-track modes that the confidence stage must report. Multi-start is therefore replaced by enumeration. The closest published structure is Argos/Transit-style positioning (one sensor, known orbits, unknown transmitter frequency). Its published error estimates were found to be overly optimistic for both its LS and Kalman algorithms ([Boyd & Brightsmith 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0063051)). That is a warning that closed-form covariances need the same calibration as everything else.

Two caveats keep the locus a seed rather than an answer. The event fit's slant range inherits drift bias. With v fixed from ADS-B, a 1 %/min drift biases d by **+6 to +51%**, which is 4–16σ ([derived_checks.md](../../research_notes/Doppler%20localization%20verification/derived_checks.md)). The fit's pruning floors (v ±20%, d within a factor of 2) are also far wider than the covariance-based σ the prototype assumed. The fix is to refit each event **conditioned on the candidate flight**: take v from ADS-B and fit f₀, β, d and t_c linearly where possible. That is the same VarPro solve used later for polish. EDT is the natural coarse score on the seeds. NonLinLoc's EDT likelihood removes the unknown origin time by scoring pairwise differences of arrival times. Its `EDT_OT_WT` variant penalises the spread of implied origin times and "leads to more compact location PDFs" ([NLLoc control docs](https://web.archive.org/web/20231207071958id_/http://alomax.free.fr/nlloc/soft7.00/control.html)). Transferred to CPA times, EDT is a sum of pairwise kernels exp(−½[(t_i − t_j) − (T_i(r) − T_j(r))]²/(σ_i² + σ_j²)), multiplied by a lateral kernel. It needs no δ grid, it costs at most 45 pairs for 10 events, and δ̂(r) is the median implied clock. Its robustness matters because association is the most error-prone stage. With the fifth of five events mistimed by 25 s, EDT peaked **21–59 m** from truth while δ-profiled L2 missed by **1.4–2.4 km** **[SIM-proto]**. The exact Lomax exponent and normalisation were not retrieved, so the kernel form is a reconstruction.

Retrieval sits in front of all of this only when it pays. Shazam's trick carries over to the clock but not to position. Shazam hashes peak pairs as (f₁, f₂, Δt) with absolute time excluded, then finds a histogram peak in the implied offset ([Wang 2003](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/papers/Wang03-shazam.pdf)). Pairs of events give clock-invariant Δt in the same way. Astrometry.net's quad codes are invariant under a transformation group ([Lang et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2233)), and receiver position does not act on Doppler observables as a group. So no location-invariant hash exists **[derived]**, and position must be generated from geometry. Each (event, flight) match votes along two strips parallel to the ground track, where the implied clock is linear in along-track position. Hough voting over (cell, clock-bin) kept the truth in **all 9 trials that ran**. The cells receiving every event's vote shrank to **0.06–7.6% of the region with 3–4 events, but only 7.7–12.8% with 2** **[SIM-proto]**. For consensus, nodes are (event, flight) pairs and edges join pairs whose loci intersect at a common clock. A maximum clique on that graph is the TEASER pattern, robust to "more than 99% outliers" in milliseconds ([Yang, Shi & Carlone 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07715)). It replaces the pairwise clock filter in `associate` and also removes the 64-hypothesis truncation risk. Because most real corpus cases appear to be single-aircraft recordings, retrieval is a busy-airspace and unknown-region tool, not a first-order accuracy lever. The 62 case directories are mostly `livermore_<tail>` and `nasa_*` names; this is an inference from directory names, not a pass count.

### Power-domain likelihood with a harmonic presence mixture and a constrained Viterbi band

The final score for each (cell, flight, event) should be a sum of per-pixel LLRs on **linear** power from one Hann window. The window is chosen per event from the predicted chirp rate. Power is normalised by a noise floor N̂ = q₂₅/ln(4/3): for Exp(1) the 0.25-quantile is 0.288 of the mean, and because a quantile commutes with log, the existing quantile floor in `audio/whiten.rs` converts directly. Ransom recommends median-style robust normalisation for the same reason ([Ransom et al. 2002 §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)). The −s term in the LLR penalises hypotheses that predict power where none is seen. That puts in-window absence on the same nats scale as presence, which a per-frame GLRT throws away. Impulsive bins should be handled by capping ℓ (for example at P = 30), not by clipping the input. The ridge-point Gaussian likelihood is the Laplace expansion of the same LLR about the per-frame peak. It is equivalent only above the frequency-estimation threshold, roughly ≥10 dB per frame **[derived]**. Separate-then-fit harmonic estimators reach the CRLB only at **≥7 dB** total-harmonic SNR ([Doweck et al., EUSIPCO 2015](https://zenodo.org/records/54339/files/1570087825.pdf)). Many corpus harmonics are probably below that, but nobody has measured the per-pixel SNR distribution yet. In the existing corpus scan's harmonic `tnr` columns, harmonics 3–6 sit near the column's own null value of about 4 ([events_summary.csv](../../research_notes/Doppler%20localization%20verification/corpus_scan/events_summary.csv)), which is suggestive but uses different units. Until the measurement exists, use power-domain scoring wherever the median per-frame z is below about 3.

Harmonics should be summed in power with weights w_h ∝ ŝ_h/(1 + ŝ_h). The sum should then be wrapped in a per-harmonic **presence mixture**, L = Σ_h log(1 − π_h + π_h·Λ_h), with π_h taken from the type tables and floored near 0.2. Ransom's pulsar results show that incoherent summing "produces worse sensitivities than not summing" when only a few harmonics carry power ([Ransom et al. 2002, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0204349)). The mixture lets a harmonic that has faded through directivity or a Lloyd's-mirror null cost about log(1 − π_h), not veto the hypothesis. Applying it per segment rather than per pass handles harmonics that fade mid-pass. The ±tol max filter should become a **Viterbi path confined to a ±1–2 bin corridor** around the predicted curve, with |Δu| ≤ 1 bin per hop. That is the HMM frequency-tracking idea from continuous-gravitational-wave searches, which "effectively sum[s] the estimator output semi-coherently" while tolerating spin wander ([Suvorova et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02412)). A track-before-detect comparison found the detection performance of grid Bayesian, Viterbi, particle and H-PMHT methods "minor" in difference for most scenarios, with cost dominated by likelihood-ratio evaluation ([EURASIP JASP 2008](https://doi.org/10.1155/2008/428036)). In this problem the state is a one-dimensional offset in a narrow tube, so the grid cost that hurt in that study does not arise. The tube also changes the role of the event catalogue. Every gated flight can be scored at θ, so a faint pass the detector missed enters as soft evidence, and `events` becomes a pruning device only, as `ARCHITECTURE.md` already intends.

Coherent integration beyond one frame is the speculative tail of this layer. Under the weak-signal approximation, semi-coherent StackSlide sensitivity scales as h_th ∝ N^{1/4}T^{-1/2} ([Prix & Shaltev 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4321)). Dechirping into longer segments therefore gains √k in power SNR per k-fold segment lengthening, which is 1.5 dB per doubling. The Doppler warp resamples audio onto the emission-time axis, so a true hypothesis becomes a constant tone. The warp is a unitary bijection ([Mao et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse12050708)), and the fan-chirp transform is its per-frame Taylor approximation ([Weruaga & Kepesi 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2007.01.006)). The phase budget is the problem. Turbulence phase rms is **0.5–1.7 rad at 1 kHz** over 0.3–3 km, with 0.5–5 s correlation times ([Rietdijk 2017](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/251306/251306.pdf)). ADS-B velocity error caps coherence near 1.7 s at 100 Hz unless a per-segment frequency slide absorbs it **[derived]**. The plausible target is 2–8 s segments at +2 to +4 dB, refine stage only, and only after aircraft-tone coherence times have been measured. No source measures those coherence times.

### Belief propagation makes association, clutter and silence one term

With labelled, known targets and one batch of measurements, the random-finite-set machinery (PHD, CPHD, PMBM, LMB) collapses to one exact expression: a labelled multi-Bernoulli likelihood plus Poisson clutter **[derived]**. Recursive filters are not needed. Two things should be borrowed. The first is the Poisson component for undetected and unknown targets ([Frohle et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.08146)). The second is the registration pattern, in which the multi-target likelihood itself is the objective over sensor parameters ([Battistelli et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02523)). In normalised form the per-cell log-likelihood is log L(θ) = Σ_j log(1 − P_d,j) + Σ_e log λ_c(e) + log Perm[ψ(θ)], with ψ_e,j = P_d,j·ℓ_e(θ|j)/((1 − P_d,j)·λ_c(e)). The **silence term now applies to every gated flight**, not only to flights left over by one chosen assignment. Applying a penalty only to "unassociated" flights double-counts or undercounts whenever the association is uncertain. The unknown-aircraft (Reid) term enters λ_c, and the earlier rounds' deaf-recording mixture wraps the whole expression. The permanent is #P-complete. Williams and Lau's scalar BP gives the marginals and an approximate log-sum in O(E·J) per iteration. Convergence is proven when P_d < 1 and clutter > 0, which holds here with P_d capped at 0.9. Marginal errors are "0.015 or less in most cases" and **0.02–0.04 at P_d = 0.9 and high SNR** ([Williams & Lau 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.6299)). That weak spot coincides with the project's P_d cap, so exact enumeration should arbitrate the top cells whenever E ≤ 5–6.

BP is cheap: about 10³ flops per cell for E ≈ 2–10 events and J ≈ 5–50 gated flights, which is negligible next to spectrogram scoring. It also produces the diagnostics the reliability gate needs: P(event e is clutter), and P(flight j was present but unheard) at the MAP. That second quantity is a strong user-facing sanity check. The Bethe log-sum's fidelity for *ranking* cells is untested, and its exact formula (Vontobel's Bethe permanent) was not retrieved, so it has to be verified against enumeration before it ranks anything. Cheap bounds come from the same matrix. The best assignment (Hungarian with clutter dummies) is a lower bound. Π_e(1 + Σ_j ψ_e,j) is an admissible upper bound, because dropping the one-to-one constraint only adds non-negative terms. Together they feed branch-and-bound over cells. The PMHT-style clutter mixture in the probabilistic note is the same likelihood written per event ([probabilistic_inference.md §1c](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/probabilistic_inference.md)). The CPD-style EM in the association note (BP marginals in the E-step, weighted Gauss–Newton in the M-step) is its continuous refinement. ICP's hard nearest-neighbour assignment is dominated by both. Sources confirmed by title only in this area include the Meyer et al. BP survey, CPD, and the Ristic–Clark–Gordon calibration paper. The closest analogue, BP with an unknown sensor state (OJSP 2022), was seen but not read.

### Layered marginalisation on a mass-driven quadtree

The per-event linear nuisances are integrated exactly. On the ridge path, f₀ and drift enter the frequency model linearly, and variable projection eliminates them per event with a 2×2 solve. The outer Levenberg–Marquardt then runs over (x, y, δ [, c, w]) only, with analytic Jacobians from the emission-time Newton solve ([Golub & Pereyra, BIT 1975](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf01932995), title only). A Gaussian prior on the drift coefficients turns profiling into marginalisation through Woodbury in O(K·p²). The log-determinant terms are the Occam factor that should select drift order p ∈ {0, 1, 2} by evidence instead of by hand ([probabilistic_inference.md §1a](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/probabilistic_inference.md)). The drift prior scales are physics, not regularisation. A free linear drift trades against pass asymmetry and v/c, and a free quadratic trades against the CPA curvature that carries range. Freeing β inflates cross-track σ by **×3.6 on a ±1 d/v span and ×1.33 on ±3 d/v** ([derived_checks.md](../../research_notes/Doppler%20localization%20verification/derived_checks.md)). On the power path, f₀ stays the existing log-axis slide, but it should be marginalised by log-sum-exp rather than maximised. Drift is absorbed partly by a small grid in the first Legendre coefficient and partly by the Viterbi corridor.

The shared nuisances split by shape. Given x and an association, δ is effectively unimodal, so it can be integrated locally around δ̂(x) (see the tensions section). The smooth perturbations c̄, w̄ and ε get a Laplace step per cell: 3–5 Gauss–Newton iterations with a 4×4 Cholesky. Each cell is spot-checked against 3-point adaptive Gauss–Hermite (81 evaluations), and cells that differ by more than 0.5 nats are flagged. Correlated residuals get an AR(1) noise model (tridiagonal inverse, O(K)), and model error is pooled by aircraft class through type-II maximum likelihood. Once those layers are in place, only x remains to be integrated numerically. The engine for that should follow NonLinLoc's oct-tree. NonLinLoc keeps every sampled cell in a list ordered by probability P_i ≈ V_i·pdf(x_i) and splits the highest-P cell until a minimum node size is reached ([NLLoc OctTree](https://web.archive.org/web/2023id_/http://alomax.free.fr/nlloc/octtree/OctTree.html)). For doppler_print that means a quadtree that keeps the smallest set of cells holding 1 − 10⁻⁴ of the mass, plus every cell within 10 nats of the maximum to protect narrow modes. Every cell stores its area, so HPD regions and mode masses are area-weighted sums. That requires `hpd_level` to take an area argument. The known failure is a narrow mode that falls between coarse cell centres. Two mitigations exist: a rigorous per-cell upper bound, obtained by scoring the centre curve on a spectrogram dilated by the cell's time and frequency Lipschitz radii, or a second run with jittered level-0 centres. The probabilistic note estimates about 10⁴ x-evaluations in total, which is comparable to today's budget, but that is an order-of-magnitude figure, not a benchmark.

A tempered SMC sampler over the full joint, hand-rolled in Rust at about 250 lines with rayon, is the independent cross-check. It is embarrassingly parallel, yields log Z for free, and supplies the posterior draws SBC needs ([Dai et al. 2022](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11936v3)). Its cost, about 1.6×10⁶ likelihood calls, belongs in `eval`, not in every run. Nested sampling via UltraNest or dynesty is the gold-standard oracle. Buchner's live-point rule, K ≳ H/min(p, 1 − p) ([Buchner 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22929)), gives K ≈ 70 for a mirror mode at p = 0.3 and H ≈ 20 nats, so K = 400 leaves margin. It belongs in a `tools/` research script that calls the dp-core likelihood through dp-py, which keeps the likelihood in Rust and the sampler out of the product. HMC (nuts-rs) cannot jump between mirror modes and needs gradients through a retarded-time solve, so it is not adopted. Neural posterior estimation is also a poor fit. The exact likelihood costs milliseconds, the conditioning set (variable ADS-B tracks plus a spectrogram) is not fixed-size, and the posterior covers about 10⁻⁵ of the prior. The one pattern worth keeping is DINGO-IS, in which a neural proposal is always reweighted by the exact likelihood. Its median sample efficiency was about 10% on 42 LIGO–Virgo events ([Dax et al. 2023](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05686v2)). The rule that follows is that no learned component enters the posterior unless the exact likelihood reweights it.

### Calibrated output: temperature by log score, conformal regions, bagging and abstention

Tempering is the right formal object. `relative_likelihood` computes L^{1/T}, and choosing T is the generalised-Bayes learning-rate problem. Wu and Martin compared selection methods and found that only Syring and Martin's coverage-targeting GPC was "more stable in achieving the coverage probability across different sample sizes and misspecification degrees". Information-matching methods (Holmes–Walker, Lyddon et al.) and SafeBayes do not target coverage, and the Lyddon et al. method tended to under-cover ([Wu & Martin 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.11349); [Syring & Martin 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.00922)). The coarsened posterior gives a physics prior for T before any calibration: η ≈ 1/(1 + n/α), with α ≈ 1/(2ε²) when systematic bias shifts residuals by ε noise-sds ([Miller & Dunson 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.06101)). Split-conformal regions with the HPD-split score fit a multimodal map natively. The score is the HPD mass needed to include the truth ([Izbicki, Shimizu & Stern 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12778)). The report then uses `hpd_level(q̂)` instead of `hpd_level(1 − α)`, and mirror pairs come out as two islands. The finite-sample cost is explicit. Conditional coverage is Beta(n + 1 − l, l), and ±0.1 slack at α = δ = 0.1 needs **n = 22 per stratum** ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)). The corpus has grown from the nine recordings the robustness note assumed to 62 case directories, about 57 of them real. Pooled real calibration at 90% is now meaningful. A 90% claim at n = 50 has only a 1.8% chance of true coverage below 80% **[derived]** ([robust_calibrated_inference.md §4](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/robust_calibrated_inference.md)). Multi-pass strata, however, still need `sim`. A prediction-powered rectifier states honestly how far real coverage may fall short of sim coverage ([Angelopoulos et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09633)).

Coherent bias is shared by every frame of a pass (c̄ by every pass), so the honest unit of independent evidence is the pass. BayesBag over passes is a reweighting of per-pass score layers, so it needs no re-search. The variance-ratio rule M = (1 + σ²/s²)N reduces to the conservative default M = N, which over-inflates by at most 2× when the model is right ([Huggins & Miller](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.07104)). The bagged-to-standard variance ratio doubles as a misspecification index. The bagged surface also reproduces thunderprint's lesson: the source count separated right from wrong answers when fit quality did not. If the answer rests on one pass, resamples that drop it move the mode. Robust losses address a different error class. Redescending estimators are "about 20% more efficient than the Huber estimator for the Cauchy distribution" because they reject gross outliers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redescending_M-estimator)). They help against wrong ridges and harmonic slips in the event fit, as a Gaussian plus uniform ε-floor mixture. They do nothing against coherent c, wind or drift bias, which is consistent with thunderprint seeing no gain from Huber at search level. The abstention gate should threshold features that carry information: distinct passes, the presence of a mirror breaker, bagged mode stability, nuisance sensitivity √λ_max(SΣ_φSᵀ), conformal area, and the clutter fraction from BP. The threshold comes from Geifman and El-Yaniv's selective-risk bound on `sim`, which they used to guarantee 2% top-5 ImageNet error with probability 99.9% at about 60% coverage ([Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08500)). Mean residual should not be a gate feature.

## Five tensions between the notes resolve into measurements

The notes disagree in places. Most of the disagreements turn out to be different quantities, or different stages, described with the same words. Each one below ends with the measurement that settles it. All the measurements run on `sim`, whose truth is independent of `physics`, plus the corpus where stated.

**f₀ drift: linear in log-frequency or linear in frequency?** The probabilistic note writes y = log h + log f₀ + g(t) + log F, with g a Legendre polynomial. That is exactly linear in (log f₀, b) for a *multiplicative* drift f₀·e^{g(t)}. The independent forward-model check found that f₀(1 + βτ) is exactly linear in f, not ln f, "because ln(1 + βτ) ≠ βτ" ([derived_checks.md](../../research_notes/Doppler%20localization%20verification/derived_checks.md)). Both statements are exact for their own drift model, so they do not contradict each other. They differ by the second-order term (βτ)²/2. At 1 %/min over ±60 s that is 5×10⁻⁵, or **0.005 Hz at 100 Hz, about 1% of the 0.39 Hz per-frame σ** **[derived, this report]**. At 3 %/min over ±60 s it is 0.045 Hz, around 12% of σ and coherent across frames. A p = 2 Legendre term absorbs it to O((βτ)³), but that is the same quadratic that trades against range. `sim` generates drift as f₀(1 + βs + jitter) ([PHYSICS.md §6](../PHYSICS.md)), so a log-domain fit sees a small structured model error in sim and none in reality, where neither form is "true". The resolution is to use each form where its algebra is natural. The log domain suits the power path, where harmonics and f₀ are shifts. The frequency domain suits VarPro ridge residuals in Hz, with Φ = [hD, hτD]. Drift is defined in centred emission time in both. The measurement runs both parameterisations on sim with β ∈ {0, 1, 3, 5} %/min and spans of ±1, 2 and 3 d/v, and compares slant bias, cross-track bias and the evidence-selected p. The prediction is that the two are indistinguishable below about 3 %/min. If so, the log form ships everywhere for simplicity.

**Frame-overlap correlation: negligible, or 2.67–5.33× variance inflation?** The two notes measure different things at different configurations. The probabilistic note computed the noise-only *power* correlation, |ρ_amp|², at 50% overlap (≈0.03) and at the 0.75 s/0.5 s setting of the detector check (0.0009). It concluded that overlap is negligible "at the project's hop". The robustness note computed the variance inflation 1 + 2Σρ_k for *linear* (amplitude or phase) quantities at 75% and 87.5% overlap (2.67 and 5.33), with power-domain equivalents of **1.92 and 3.85** ([robust_calibrated_inference.md §2](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/robust_calibrated_inference.md)). The actual search plane is a 1 s window at a 0.25 s hop, which is 75% overlap ([spectrogram.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/audio/spectrogram.rs)). The discovery front end max-combines 0.5, 1 and 2 s windows at the same hop, which is 50–87.5%. For a power-LLR sum, the right figure is therefore about 1.9×, not "negligible". For ridge-frequency residuals, which behave closer to linear phase quantities and are further smoothed by peak interpolation and window max-combination, inflation is 2.7× or more. That agrees with `fit.rs` already measuring the lag-1 ridge autocorrelation and inflating its covariance by (1 + ρ)/(1 − ρ). A third quantity sits on top: correlated *model error* (CV approximation, drift, atmosphere, track interpolation), which persists for seconds. The probabilistic note is right that model error, not overlap, drives the over-confidence `likelihood.rs` complains about. The settling experiment has three arms. (a) Noise-only sim: compute the empirical Var(Σ pixels)/(n·Var) along fixed off-signal curves for the actual plane. (b) A perfect-model tone (straight CV, still air, no drift, jitter or ground): compute the ACF of ridge residuals against truth and the variance of the event score across noise seeds. (c) Switch on jitter, drift, wind and ground reflection one at a time; the extra ACF in each case is the model-error component (σ_model, ρ_model). The outputs are a deterministic n_eff factor per STFT configuration and an AR(1) model-error prior. Whatever over-dispersion remains is left for T.

**Fine δ grid, or EDT with no δ at all?** The verification found that the δ grid must keep Δδ/2 below the timing tolerance and must be incommensurate with Δs/v. Curve-sum scores tolerate **0.48–0.96 s**, but the likelihood reaches Δχ² = 1 at **0.07 s**, and a commensurate step is the worst choice ([derived_checks.md](../../research_notes/Doppler%20localization%20verification/derived_checks.md)). EDT says δ needs no grid. Both hold, at different stages. EDT removes δ from the *coarse CPA-time* score and returns δ̂(x) together with its spread. The final spectrogram likelihood still depends on δ. However, given x and an association, δ is pinned near δ̂(x) and is unimodal. The multimodality the probabilistic note warns about lives in the joint (x, δ) ridge, which the quadtree resolves in x, and in association-induced clock alternatives, which BP or enumeration sums as separate components. δ can therefore be integrated locally by Laplace, or by quadrature over ±3σ_δ with a step no larger than σ_δ/2. The incommensurability trap disappears because δ no longer sits on a lattice coupled to the cell lattice. One consequence for today's code follows **[derived, this report]**. Its 0.25 s clock step is fine for the curve-sum score. For a likelihood with 0.07 s tolerance it is too coarse: a grid maximum can sit up to 0.125 s off, which costs up to about 1.6 nats per sharply timed event. Predicted curves therefore need sub-frame time shifts, which is cheap because `doppler_factors` takes arbitrary times. The FFT over δ (and over δ × f₀) belongs only in the "no timestamp" mode, where the clock prior spans minutes. The measurement compares, on sim cells, the log L(x) from local Laplace-δ against dense 0.01 s quadrature over ±10 s, and flags any cell that differs by more than 0.5 nats.

**Peak score, or basin mass?** `eval` ranks flight tuples by peak score, and `pipeline` refines by peak rank. A mirror mode with an equal peak but a sharper basin holds less posterior mass. Mass is the Occam-correct quantity: under Laplace, P_m ∝ L̂_m·π(x̂_m)·|H_m|^{−1/2} with the nuisances included ([probabilistic_inference.md §5](../../research_notes/Doppler%20matching%20mathematical%20methods/probabilistic_inference.md)). Mass depends on T, though, since the basin-width factor scales as |H/T|^{−1/2}. That makes mass ranking meaningful only after T is calibrated, which is why calibrated output comes before mass ranking in the roadmap. The two rankings should always be reported side by side, and a large disagreement between them flags a ridge. The measurement uses sim mirror and along-track pairs and multi-tuple cases. It compares the log loss of mode choice under peak ranking against mass ranking at the fitted T, and adopts mass if it wins in a paired test.

**Power LLR or ridge Woodbury as the primary likelihood; conformal or a calibrated T.** These are complements, not rivals. The power LLR is the final score. The ridge-Gaussian likelihood, which is its above-threshold Laplace form, drives seeding, VarPro-LM and local covariances. The switch rule (median per-frame z below about 3) should be checked against the per-pixel SNR distribution of real corpus events, which nobody has measured yet. Conformal calibration fixes the *mass level* that is reported. T still sets the *shape*: mode masses, island balance and region outline. So T is fitted first by maximising the mean log score at truth, which is strictly proper and cheap because a cached surface is simply re-tempered. Conformal q̂ is then computed per stratum. GPC is needed only if coverage-against-nominal curves show level-dependent miscalibration, which would mean the shape is wrong. The Viterbi corridor creates one more tension that needs watching. A corridor wide enough to absorb RPM wander can also absorb the geometric shift that discriminates neighbouring cells. Its width therefore has to be set by measuring likelihood curvature against cell offset for tube widths of 0, ±1, ±2 and ±3 bins.

## Adopt in nine stages, measurement first

The ranking weighs expected accuracy gain against effort and the risk of silent regressions. Measurement comes first because no variant can be promoted without it. Changes that make the likelihood correct come before changes that make the search faster. Every experiment is one `experiments/*.toml`, pre-registered with a primary metric. Each variant is compared at **its own best-fit T** so that the temperature does not confound the comparison. Expected gains are targets drawn from the notes, not results.

Several candidates are deliberately left out. PHD, CPHD, PMBM and MHT filters spend their complexity on recursion and unknown target states that this problem does not have. SDP relaxations for TDOA/FDOA target multi-sensor geometry and were confirmed by title only. Their remaining non-convexity here is the mirror sign plus c, which enumeration and a 1-D scan handle exactly. Kennedy–O'Hagan GP discrepancy is confounded with the physical parameters (the Brynjarsdóttir and O'Hagan text was blocked) when four physical nuisances are known. SafeBayes and Holmes–Walker do not target coverage. ABC only adds tolerance error to an explicit likelihood. The 2-D (δ, f₀) FFT and the precomputed CV template tables wait until stage 0's profile shows that curve evaluation dominates runtime. The rule that `sim` must not reuse `physics::doppler_factors` constrains three components. The warp, the template tables and the locus all live in `search`. SBC also only certifies what `sim` can falsify. Until `sim` gains stratified c(z) and w(z), the stage 5 SBC will pass trivially on atmospheric marginalisation, and the misspecified scenario bank will be testing only what it injects.

## Conclusion

Every component in the target architecture rewrites part of the score as a log-probability on a common scale, so that terms which today are separately tuned heuristics can be added. These are the pixel LLR, the harmonic mixture, the clutter and silence terms, the drift Occam factor and the mode mass. The current search mixes z-units, a fixed penalty and a fixed temperature, so no single change can be judged in isolation. The calibration stage, not any one new model, therefore unlocks the rest of the roadmap. It also changes the meaning of "better". Peak rank and margin are improper metrics for a multimodal answer. The roadmap's success criteria use log score, coverage and recall at each stage, because those are the quantities a new likelihood can move without moving the headline error.

The tensions show that the notes' numbers are more fragile than their disagreements suggest. The overlap figure flips from "negligible" to about 2× depending on which STFT configuration and which quantity is measured. The δ-step guidance changes by an order of magnitude between curve-sum and likelihood scoring. The drift parameterisation matters only above a few percent per minute. Every [SIM-proto] result (the 8–20 m locus, the robust EDT and the 9-for-9 retrieval) comes from straight tracks in still air, built on assumptions that `sim` shares. The cheapest high-value next step is therefore stage 0 together with the five settling measurements, run before any new modelling code is written. That gives each subsequent change a pre-registered target that it can fail.
