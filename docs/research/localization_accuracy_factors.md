# Geometry and sound speed set the error floor

**doppler_print's accuracy is limited first by geometry and the atmosphere, and only after that by signal processing.** Satellite-Doppler work from the 1970s, single-observer Doppler bounds, and the project's own derivations agree that a single straight pass determines only four numbers: rest frequency, v/c, CPA slant range and CPA time. That leaves three gaps. There is an exact trade-off between along-track position and clock offset. There is a two-fold mirror across the ground track. And because the curve is invariant under scaling (c, v, d) together, any relative error in sound speed passes one-for-one into range. Given that structure, the largest correctable biases are a wrong path-mean sound speed and an ignored along-track wind. The researchers' first-order simulations put these at **20–70 m for low GA, 30–265 m for GA or approach traffic at 600–1000 m, and 150–800 m for jets at 2 km**. That is above the search's 100 m grid. ADS-B errors (about 30 m median position, roughly 0.1–0.5 s timestamp bias), consumer clock drift (tens of ppm), codecs, ground reflection, turbulence and absorption each contribute tens of metres or less in the typical case. They matter mainly through their tails: GNSS glitches of hundreds of kilometres, a mislabelled sample rate, or noise suppression that deletes faint tones. Most of the remaining accuracy has to come from inference design, not better physics. The biggest gains are expected from five changes. In order: (1) feed a path-averaged sound speed and wind into the uniform-medium slot that `docs/PHYSICS.md` already has; (2) treat clock offset, sound speed, wind and clock rate as nuisances shared by all events, and marginalise them rather than maximising them; (3) report mirror and along-track modes explicitly; (4) turn the silence penalty into a capped (1 − P_d) likelihood; (5) measure calibration on `sim`. Most numbers below that are tagged **[derived]** or **[SIM]** come from back-of-envelope or straight-ray scripts the researchers wrote this session. They are not literature results, and none of them has yet been reproduced with the project's independent `sim` crate.

## A single pass pins only four numbers, so geometry dominates

The geometry was solved decades ago in satellite Doppler positioning. Argentiero and Marini showed that the single-pass least-squares problem "yields conjugate solutions on either side of the satellite subtrack". They constrained the transmitter to the ellipsoid because recovering three position components from one pass is very difficult, and they found that the carrier frequency "cannot be assumed as known" ([NASA TM-78049](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780011233/downloads/19780011233.pdf)). That maps directly onto doppler_print's setup: a DEM ground constraint plus a per-event f0. The same paper gives the operational rule for the mirror choice. Residual covariances at the true and mirror points are **"nearly identical"**, so curvature cannot separate them; only the mean residual can. Picking the solution with the smaller loss is the most powerful test only when systematic errors (sound speed, wind, latency, DEM) are included in the observation covariance Φ = BPBᵀ + Q ([NASA TM-78049](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780011233/downloads/19780011233.pdf)). If those errors are ignored, picking the larger score is not the optimal test. Nuisance parameters are costly. Li et al. computed a Doppler-only bound of **≈381 m** for a single moving RF observer. Adding periodic time-of-arrival, which removes the timing nuisance, cut it to **≈128 m**. They also derive the Schur-complement form of the position bound with nuisances ([Li et al.](https://thomasez.folk.ntnu.no/itc34/workshop%20papers/1.pdf)). Equal Doppler histories require equal rest tonals ([arXiv 2507.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14765v1)), so a type-specific f0 prior is a legitimate way to break association ambiguity. For Argos, motion-model priors helped most when a pass carried three or fewer messages ([Lopez et al. 2015](https://biomedcentral-animalbiotelemetry.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/counter/pdf/10.1186/s40317-015-0073-4.pdf)). Boyd and Brightsmith found no such gain on their collar data ([PLoS ONE 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3656847/)). The likely reconciliation is that priors pay off only when individual passes are weak.

Applied to the project's constant-velocity model, the researchers derived several consequences of this structure. **[derived]** Shifting the receiver by Δs along the track and the clock by Δs/v leaves the curve unchanged, so the Fisher matrix is exactly singular in that direction. Only turns or a second event break it. **[derived]** A metadata time prior of width σ_δ is therefore an along-track prior of width v·σ_δ; 10 s at 70 m/s is 700 m. **[derived]** Cross-track error is amplified by d/|y| near overhead. For h = 1000 m and d = 1050 m, a 30 m height error (baro versus geometric, or a geoid slip) becomes **about 94 m** of ground offset. The project's rule never to mix height datums is therefore worth roughly 100 m on near-overhead passes. **[derived]** The scale invariance (v, d, c) → k(v, d, c) holds exactly for the `docs/PHYSICS.md` §5 closed form. A 3% sound-speed error is therefore a 3% error in v and d at any SNR. `AUDIO.md`'s `c_rel_unc` widening is necessary, and only independent information (ADS-B ground speed, temperature) can remove the error.

The Cramér–Rao table the frequency-estimation researcher computed **[derived, `crlb.py`]** changes the priorities for the event stage. It assumes f0 = 100 Hz, v = 80 m/s, 0 dB per 1 s frame and independent frames, so it is optimistic by about √(overlap factor). The (v, d) pair is the weak direction, and observed span and two-sidedness control accuracy far more than per-frame precision at CPA.

| Observed ridge | σd | σf0 | r(v,d) |
|---|---|---|---|
| d = 1.5 km, ±60 s | 20 m | 0.06 Hz | 0.88 |
| d = 1.5 km, ±60 s, CPA ±8 s missing | 23 m | 0.07 Hz | 0.90 |
| d = 1.5 km, ±20 s | 93 m | 0.22 Hz | 0.992 |
| d = 1.5 km, receding only (0–60 s) | 266 m | 2.8 Hz | 0.986 |
| d = 4 km, ±20 s | 2333 m | 0.94 Hz | 0.999 |

Losing the CPA neighbourhood costs about 15%. Losing one asymptote costs **13–50×**, and f0, v and d become collinear (r(f0, v) ≈ +0.999). Two consequences follow for the event stage. First, `AUDIO.md` treats event bounds as independent ±3σ boxes, which are loose on the minor axis and can still clip the major axis of a ridge that is truly 0.99-correlated. Second, a truncated event should not carry f0 as a free parameter; take f0 from the harmonic family, a type prior or ADS-B speed instead. Because event bounds only prune, the real risk is losing the truth at the association stage, which `eval` already reports as "the stage where the truth was lost".

## Sound speed and along-track wind are the largest physical biases

The atmospheric literature splits cleanly. Single-sensor Doppler flight-parameter work (Ferguson, Lo, Yu et al.) assumes a constant c. Yu et al. use 340 m/s in air and 1520 m/s in water, with no atmospheric variability ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.996493/pdf)). Explicit atmospheric modelling lives in noise prediction and tomography instead. Arntzen et al. integrate travel time along curved rays and obtain Doppler as its time derivative, the same F = dτ/dt idea as `PHYSICS.md` §3. In a measured flyover, refraction was **"primarily caused by wind gradients rather than temperature gradients"**. The listener sat in an upwind shadow zone for the first 36 s, and multiple eigenrays appeared downwind after about 102 s. Outside shadow zones, rays "nearly follow the straight line ray paths" ([Arntzen et al., NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120010289/downloads/20120010289.pdf)). Finn and Rogers show the same signals are big enough to invert. They recover boundary-layer temperature and wind from UAV engine-tone Doppler to **better than 0.5 °C and 0.3 m/s** against lidar ([ISPRS 2019](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W13/283/2019/isprs-archives-XLII-2-W13-283-2019.pdf)). In other words, atmospheric structure is signal-sized, not noise. Historical inputs are adequate but not exact: Mode-S EHS winds are good to **≈1.1–2.5 m/s** ([AMT 2016](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/9/4141/2016/amt-9-4141-2016.pdf)), and HRRR has **2–3 m/s RMSE** in the 20–500 m layer ([NOAA 25479](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/25479)). Plain ADS-B carries no weather data. EHS registers exist only where a Mode-S radar interrogates ([Mirza et al.](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/65960/1/MirzaEtAl_AuthorFinalVersion.pdf)).

The effect sizes below are all **[SIM]**. They come from `bias_sim.py` and `ground_abs.py`: a first-order straight-ray layered-medium model, noise-free, f0 = 100 Hz, with the observer fitted using absolute time known. They are not field results.

| Effect, if ignored | GA, 300 m alt | GA/approach, 600–1000 m | Jet, 2 km | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Path-mean c wrong by 6 K | 20 m | 32–63 m | 155 m | Model |
| Fixed 343 m/s on a 0 °C day (≈20 K) | 69 m | 111–265 m | 801 m | Model |
| ISA lapse ignored, ground c correct | 3 m | 11–35 m | 175 m | Model for jets |
| +10 K inversion over 0–300 m | 17 m | 42–83 m | 201 m | Model via profile |
| 10 m/s along-track wind | 11 m | 27–35 m | 317 m | Supply w̄ |
| Shear 0→15 m/s, surface wind used | 3.5 m | 12–16 m | 205 m | Use profile mean |
| 10 m/s cross-track wind | <1 m | <1 m | <1 m | Ignore |
| Ground reflection, mic 1.5–10 m | ≤2 m | ≤5 m | ≤7 m | Tracker gaps only |

The most important qualitative finding is that atmospheric error hides. **[SIM]** A 10 m/s along-track wind on the GA case leaves only 53 mHz of rms residual, because most of it is absorbed into a wrong f0 (−0.54 Hz) and a wrong range. Sound-speed errors leave larger residuals (0.07–0.8 Hz). Fit quality alone therefore cannot flag the problem. Multi-event consistency can, because a wrong w biases flights on reciprocal headings in opposite directions. **[SIM]** With a free clock offset rather than known absolute time, c and w errors leak into along-track position through the retarded time (about v·R·δc/c²). The free-offset fits were ill-conditioned, with shifts from tens of metres to over 1 km. That is exactly the regime doppler_print operates in.

The prescription is cheap. **[derived]** Because of Fermat's principle, a straight chord with the correct travel-time-preserving mean c̄ (the harmonic mean of c(z)) and the path-weighted wind w̄ is correct to first order. Full ray tracing is only needed for grazing segments. Samples below about 10–15° elevation or beyond 2–3 km slant should be down-weighted rather than modelled. The uniform-w slot in `PHYSICS.md` §2–3 already accepts w̄. **[SIM]** Using the surface (METAR-style) wind removes about ½–⅔ of the shear bias. The rest needs winds at aircraft altitude. The researchers also propose an untested source of temperature: the hypsometric difference between ADS-B geometric and baro altitude gives a column-mean temperature anomaly with no weather download.

The other propagation effects do not move the location. ISO 9613-1 absorption is 0.22 dB/km at 100 Hz and 4.98 dB/km at 1 kHz (20 °C, 70% RH). It rises to 36 dB/km at 2 kHz on a cold, dry day ([Sengpiel/ISO 9613-1](https://sengpielaudio.com/AirdampingFormula.htm)). It affects amplitude and which harmonics survive, not frequency. Turbulence decorrelates tones and "haystacks" them into sidebands ([Clair & Gabard](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/418892/1/Clair_Gabard_JFM_2018_postprint.pdf); [Rietdijk thesis](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/251306/251306.pdf)). **[SIM]** At Rietdijk's parameters, phase rms is 0.05–0.17 rad at 100 Hz and saturates at 1 kHz over 3 km. That is zero-mean IF jitter of 0.02–0.5 Hz which averages out, and it is an argument for fitting the low harmonics. Lloyd's-mirror fringes in flyover spectrograms are real ([TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_9a77e490-1b2e-45f2-9699-ecf03914b4c8)), and certification practice avoids them with ground-plane microphones ([US 4625828](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4625828)). Their damage is to tracking continuity, not bias. **[derived]** At 1.5 m mic height the notch spacing near CPA is about 114 Hz, so a 100 Hz fundamental can fade exactly at CPA. That is one more reason to sum harmonics. Building echoes are different: a wall produces a separate, differently Doppler-shifted ghost ridge, which acts as a decoy.

One SIM finding conflicts with the DSP note and needs isolating. On the jet case, a 2 s averaging window gave about +40 m of cross-track bias against +2 m at 0.5 s. The frequency researcher's analytic estimate puts window bias at 0.003–0.03 Hz for GA-like curves, because the second derivative f″ vanishes at CPA. Both can be true, since jet curves are much sharper. The Q = 0, 2 s control run that would separate window bias from reflection was not done.

## ADS-B, recorder and source errors are small except in their tails

ADS-B position is accurate to a few tens of metres. The NATS GA trial measured **median horizontal error of 34 m (GA) and 28 m (certified)** against a radar tracker. The tails were **269 km and 216 km**, each traced to a single airframe ([NATS 2015](https://nats.aero/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/GA-ADS-B-GPS-Trial-report.pdf)). NIC appears in every position message, whereas NACp arrives only every ~2.5 s ([NAVI](https://navi.ion.org/content/72/3/navi.716)). The FAA notes that "once NIC falls below 6, the most likely value to be reported is NIC=0" ([FAA IP/13 2024](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/APAC/Meetings/2024/2024%20SURICG-9/4-Information%20Papers/IP13_USA-AI.6-ADS-B-Equipage-and-Quality-Performance.pdf)). That makes NIC the natural per-point quality gate. The velocity categories are coarse: NACv 2 means under 3 m/s at 95% ([mode-s.org](https://mode-s.org/1090mhz/content/ads-b/7-uncertainty.html)). **[derived]** Because d ∝ v², 1 m/s of speed error at 50 m/s is a **4% range error**, so reported ground speed should be a prior with σ ≈ 1–1.5 m/s rather than truth. That is consistent with the existing free-speed-within-5-kt band.

Avionics latency is tightly bounded. Per-track mean uncompensated latency in Chicago STARS data was within ±60 ms ([arXiv 2008.06352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06352)). In crowd data, though, the receiver adds the timestamp, and about 80% of OpenSky feeders are non-GPS RTL-SDR units ([arXiv 2012.00116](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.00116); [Olive et al. 2025](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/joas/article/view/7882)). No aggregator publishes a timestamp error figure. **[derived]** The researchers' working prior is ±0.5 s per aircraft, which is **25–30 m along-track for GA and 60–115 m for jets**. A common bias is absorbed by the shared clock offset. Biases that differ per aircraft are not, and they become inconsistencies between events. Crowd MLAT has a median error of about 166 m ([Schäfer et al. 2014](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/7238/ipsn2014.pdf)), and it is nearly absent at low altitude, so MLAT and TIS-B tracks belong in silence modelling and candidate generation, not precise fitting. **[derived]** On interpolation: a standard-rate GA turn rotates the velocity 12° across a 4 s gap. Using the chord direction would then mis-predict v_r by up to 10 m/s, about 3 Hz at 100 Hz. `PHYSICS.md`'s cubic Hermite with reported velocities as tangents already avoids this. An RTS smoother fed with NIC/NACp-derived σ would add outlier rejection and a covariance on top.

The recording chain matters mostly through timing. Hand-held devices drift by **up to 60 ms/min** (≈1000 ppm) relative to each other ([Guggenberger et al. 2015](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/an-analysis-of-time-drift-in-hand-held-recording-devices/2361408)), and most crystals are specified at ≤100 ppm ([USPTO 7680285](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7680285)). **[derived]** Within one pass, a constant rate error rescales f0 and leaves v exactly unbiased. The effect on d is about 1e-4 relative, about 300× below `c_rel_unc`. Across a file it accumulates as ε·T: 0.36 s/h at 100 ppm and 3.6 s/h at 1000 ppm. Hour-long files therefore need t_utc = t0 + (1+ε)·t_audio. A mislabelled 44.1/48 kHz rate is an 8.8% stretch that breaks multi-event matching. Mains hum at a non-nominal frequency exposes it. YouTube's itag 140 (AAC, 44.1 kHz) and 251 (Opus, 48 kHz, transcoded from the AAC) both preserve the band below 1 kHz best ([audiomisc.co.uk](https://audiomisc.co.uk/YouTube/SpotTheDifference.html)). The real codec risk is masking-driven spectral holes in faint tones, and nobody has published a figure for it. ENF gives offset, rate and grid from a 1 s public log such as NESO ([NESO](https://www.neso.energy/data-portal/system-frequency-data)). However, in battery-powered recordings hum is mostly acoustic, coming from nearby mains equipment ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288628256_Source_of_ENF_in_battery-powered_digital_recordings)), so it will often be missing outdoors. **[derived]** A visible aircraft is an unexploited range measurement: d = c·Δ between the visual and acoustic CPA, giving σ_d ≈ 34 m for σ_Δ = 0.1 s.

On the source side, the main issue is drift. f0 drift is partly degenerate with geometry. Fixed-pitch props shed about 8.5% rpm for a 20% airspeed loss ([Kitplanes](https://www.kitplanes.com/wind-tunnel-55/)). Synchrophasers have only 25 rpm of authority ([MT-Propeller](https://mt-propeller.com/userdata/Service-Documents/Operation-Manuals/e-2565.pdf)). Unsynchronised twins split the blade-pass line ([Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/the-impact-of-asynchronous-propeller-speeds-on-aeroacoustics-and-/)). Modern helicopters vary rotor speed between about 96% and 103.5% automatically ([Airbus H160, ERF](https://dspace-erf.nlr.nl/server/api/core/bitstreams/44813b1f-aa38-4672-a825-0fd80f7b6c25/content)), and jet fan BPF tracks N1, which is estimated per flyover ([Merino-Martínez et al.](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/calculation-of-the-fan-rotational-speed-based-on-flyover-recordin)). **[derived]** A 1%-per-minute drift biases free-fit range by about β·c·d/v². That is **2.3% at 1 km and 50 m/s, 12% at 5 km, and 32% for a 30 m/s helicopter at 5 km**: the bias is worst exactly for distant, slow aircraft. In the search stage the geometry comes from ADS-B, so a linear β enters ln f linearly and can be solved in closed form per cell without breaking identifiability. That only holds if β is parameterised in centred emission time and the ridge spans at least ±(2–3)·d/v around CPA.

## The estimator and likelihood leave avoidable losses

Two weaknesses in the current front end are documented in the literature. Candan's windowed three-sample interpolator reaches **MSE/CRB = 1.68 (rectangular) and 3.51 (Hamming)** with no bias floor ([Candan 2015](https://users.metu.edu.tr/ccandan/pub_dir/freq_estimation_with_windowing_Elsevier_SP_2015.PDF)). Murakami and Wang extend this exactly to cosine-sum windows such as Hann ([ICASSP 2020](https://personalpages.surrey.ac.uk/w.wang/papers/MurakamiW_ICASSP_2020.pdf)). `candidate.rs` instead applies a parabolic fit to a whitened row that is clipped to [−3, 8], max-pooled from linear bins, and max-combined across three windows. **[derived, untested]** Each of those steps pulls the estimate toward bin centres. The resulting picket-fence bias is correlated along the track, so neither the AR(1) inflation nor MAD scaling removes it. The fix is to refine on the complex linear STFT of one window chosen from the predicted chirp rate. Separately, harmonic-locked fitting is CRB-efficient for harmonic chirps ([Doweck et al. 2015](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/parameter-estimation-of-harmonic-linear-chirps/)). **[derived]** Fitting harmonics 1–4 jointly at equal SNR improves f0 and geometry precision by √30 ≈ **5.5×** over refining on "the strongest harmonic". It also removes the discontinuity when directivity changes which harmonic is strongest mid-pass.

At low SNR the architecture matters more than the interpolator. Below threshold, frame-wise frequency errors become outliers spread across the band ([Quinn & Kootsookos](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/threshold-behavior-of-the-maximum-likelihood-estimator-of-frequen/)). Model-based dechirp acquisition reaches the joint frequency/chirp-rate bound at −5 dB per sample, and its threshold depends on the number of effective search cells ([Wei et al., arXiv 2608.01756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01756)). Gravitational-wave HMMs that add phase continuity match fully coherent sensitivity ([arXiv 1710.07092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.07092)). **[derived]** Because of that cell-count dependence, the ADS-B-predicted curve, with t_cpa fixed by the flight, has a detection threshold several dB lower than free discovery. That supports the existing design choice that **the search's curve-sum score is the primary likelihood and `discover` only seeds and prunes**. It also means pruning must be lenient on faint events. The minimal upgrade is to turn the curve-sum into a per-pixel log-likelihood ratio under the χ² null of whitened power. That makes it a proper GLRT and gives silence and event terms a common scale.

The statistical framework comes from search theory and tracking. Stone et al.'s AF447 search used the (1 − p_d) update per unsuccessful search and capped sensor P_d at 0.9 because manufacturer estimates "tend to be optimistic". The wreck lay in an area the passive acoustic search had "thoroughly covered", because the beacons had failed. The authors concluded that the right approach would have been a **joint distribution on beacon failure and location**, marginalised afterwards ([Stone et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.4720)). The analogue for doppler_print's silence stage is a latent recording-level "deaf" state, covering indoor capture, noise suppression, high-pass filtering or wind masking. Without it, silence can veto the true cell, as the unlisted FBI and DHS aircraft in the prior-art report already warn. For associating events with flights, the PDAF/JPDA tradition ([Bar-Shalom et al., NJIT](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/12313)) suggests summing over the k best assignments, with an explicit clutter option for each event, instead of committing to the single best flight. Student-t residuals add one robustness knob ([Tresp & Briegel](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/1999/file/db29450c3f5e97f97846693611f98c15-Paper.pdf)). Simulation-based calibration checks whether reported regions mean what they claim ([Talts et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788v2)). **[derived]** Two problems in the current search follow. The locked joint score profiles (maximises) the clock offset; marginalising it adds the Occam penalty against loosely constrained cells. And a clock-offset grid coarser than Δs/v (**2.9 s for 200 m cells at 70 m/s**) chops the long, thin along-track ridge into disconnected spots that the coarse max-pool under-ranks.

## What must be checked in `sim` before it is trusted

None of the location-bias magnitudes in this report has a published counterpart. The researchers found **no paper reporting aircraft-Doppler localisation error against wind or temperature**, no closed-form Fisher matrix for the finite-c retarded-time curve, no measured detection loss for faint tones under AAC/Opus, no published "ambiguity collapse versus number of passes" curve, and no head-to-head comparison of GLRT and ridge-then-fit on aircraft audio. The derivation scripts behind the [SIM] and [derived] tables (`crlb.py`, `bias_sim.py`, `ground_abs.py`) live in the session scratchpad at `/tmp/claude-1001/-srv-agents-work-doppler-print/f351fe27-92e0-4139-9299-afa3489c9a2b/scratchpad/`. That directory is temporary, so copy anything worth keeping before it is cleared. The scripts also share a first-order straight-ray truth model with the fitted model, which is exactly the independence `PHYSICS.md` §6 forbids for `sim`. The verification path is to add layered c(z) and w(z), β drift, a 2 s/Q = 0 window control, codec round-trips and N_events = 1…5 sweeps as `experiments/*.toml` ablations, then compare `tools/dev.sh eval` rows against the tables above. If the `sim` numbers come out within a factor of about 2 of the SIM tables, items 1, 9 and 10 can proceed as defaults. If not, the ranking should be redone from the `eval` output.

## Conclusion

The accuracy question has changed shape. The prior-art work framed the open problems as ambiguity and confidence. This round shows they are tied to the atmosphere. Sound speed and along-track wind create biases that are both large and nearly invisible in single-event residuals. With a free clock, the same errors also leak into along-track position. The fixes are therefore coupled: one joint likelihood that shares c̄, w̄, clock offset and clock rate across events both removes the geometric degeneracies and makes atmospheric error observable, much as Finn and Rogers turned it into a measurement. Jets at altitude are where this pays most. They are also the case the project flags rather than drops, so flagged jets should still contribute to estimating the shared atmosphere.

The second shift concerns where the effort should go. Most DSP refinements change event bounds, and event bounds only prune. Their value lies in not losing the truth at association, not in sharpening the final fix. The final fix depends on the search likelihood: per-pixel GLRT scoring, marginalised nuisances, capped negative evidence with a "deaf recording" escape hatch, and a calibration harness that shows whether a 90% region means 90%. None of these needs new data sources, and all of them can be tested on `sim` before any real recording is involved.
