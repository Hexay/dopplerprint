# Verification moves the bottleneck into dp-core's front end

**Most of the physics in doppler_print's three earlier research reports survives full-text checks, independent re-derivation and new simulation. The ranking of what to fix does not survive.** The largest bias measured this round is not atmospheric. It comes from dp-core's own event front end: `peak_near` reads ridge frequency off a whitened, clipped, max-combined image, and that inflates fitted CPA range by **+2 to +40 %**, speed by up to +15 % and CPA time by up to +4 s on clean simulated passes. Refining on the complex STFT (Candan) removes it entirely, at a cost of about 50 lines **[MEASURED-dp-core]**. The second correction concerns f0 drift. Its bias is 2–15× the earlier first-order formula, and the formula predicts the wrong sign. The search also has no drift term, so a 1 %/min rpm drift moves the located receiver **150 m cross-track at 1.5 km and 700–900 m at 5 km** **[MEASURED-dp-core]**. The third concerns the mirror ambiguity. On a straight pass, Doppler cannot break it at any frequency resolution: P(correct) is exactly 0.5. A constant-rate turn breaks it only as well as the sound speed is known, and a 3 % error in c produces a confident wrong mirror **[derived]**. On the atmosphere, the earlier prescription holds with one fix. A straight chord with the height-mean sound speed and the height-mean **wind vector** (not the along-track scalar) removes 93–96 % of shear bias in an exact moving-medium ray trace **[SIM]**. The earlier "inversion shadow" was a misreading: those rays turn above the aircraft and still arrive. Codecs turn out to be mostly harmless to propeller tones, while the decoders matter more: symphonia cannot open Opus at all and leaves a 1024-sample AAC lag. The real corpus is 57 recordings, not 9. It holds no metadata time, no true stereo and no traction or GSM cues, but it does contain a hidden dataset clock for 32 of the 40 Livermore cases. The merged roadmap therefore puts the event-front-end fix, a drift term and discovery/association robustness ahead of atmospheric modelling. Stereo, region masks and the elevation gate move down.

## 1. Headline changes

Eight results change what the project should do next. **(a) The event front end is biased.** `candidate.rs::peak_near` has a flat-top tie-break and runs on a whitened image. Together these add +0.5–0.8 bins on strong lines and 0.4–0.9 bins of track-correlated error on slow ridges. AF's "picket-fence pull toward bin centres" turns out to be the wrong mechanism, and its fix (Candan on the complex STFT) is the right one. **(b) f0 drift is the largest source-side bias, and the search ignores it.** **(c) Mirror resolution needs a turn entry, a second pass or a non-Doppler cue.** More frequency precision cannot do it on a straight pass, and any mirror-mode mass must treat c as a prior-constrained nuisance, never as a fixed value. **(d) Wind enters as the z-mean horizontal vector.** A crosswind under shear shifts the zero-Doppler point 36–139 m along track, so AF's "cross-track wind: ignore" is wrong once shear exists. **(e) The inversion "shadow" in DF is source-end grazing loss, not silence.** Turning rays fill it, so the silence term must not penalise it. **(f) The recording chain is benign for frequencies but hostile to amplitude and to stereo.** Phone noise suppression lowers tone and noise together, Opus collapses low-frequency inter-channel phase, and the corpus contains no real stereo to test against. **(g) The corpus is bigger but poorer in cues than assumed.** It holds 57 real recordings with no metadata clocks, and single-line region detectors fire falsely on 15–38 % of US files. **(h) Discovery and association, not codecs or atmosphere, are where measured end-to-end runs fail.** With discovered rather than oracle events, the search failed or missed by about 5 km in 10 of 12 codec runs.

## 3. What changed, area by area

### Physics and atmosphere: the z-mean wind vector works; the night branch is still open

The new ray tracer is exact for stratified moving media. It solves the eigenray as the unique maximiser of a concave Legendre functional and gets Doppler as F = 1/(1 − s_h·u) with no numerical differentiation. It reproduces `PHYSICS.md`'s uniform closed forms to 7e-9 in F and agrees with an independent Hamiltonian ODE to 2 µs ([wind_shear_raytrace.md](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization verification/wind_shear_raytrace.md>)). Across 460 cases (log-law, mixed-layer and two LLJ profiles; GA at 300 and 1000 m, jets at 2 and 3 km; receivers 0.2–5 km off track), fitting the uniform model with the **height-mean wind vector over [receiver DEM height, aircraft height] removes 93–96 % of the median CPA-range bias and 88–98 % of the f0 bias** **[SIM]**. That leaves 1–7 m median. The rest concentrates in two degraded regimes. Under a 20 m/s LLJ the residual is 25–38 m for jets and 53–72 m for GA1000 at 5 km. With strong shear just below a jet's altitude it is 20–38 m, plus a 0.2–0.3 % f0 bias. The residual also grows 2.4–3× per doubling of wind, so the fix weakens on exactly the strong-LLJ nights when reanalysis wind is least reliable. A ray-path-weighted wind (an oracle here) cuts those cases another 2–5×. It is worth implementing only for nocturnal LLJ and for GA beyond about 2 km offset. Two earlier results change. The along-track scalar is not enough: a crosswind under shear shifts the zero-Doppler point by up to 139 m (jets), and only the cross-track component of the vector removes that. And a free clock does not amplify wind bias. It absorbs the along-track part one-for-one, which is consistent with the exact degeneracy.

The **"62–70 % in shadow under a +10 K inversion"** figure in DF §2 was a misreading. Classifying where the limiting ray grazes separates two cases. Receiver-end grazing is a true upward-refraction shadow: the upwind half of a GA300 pass under log-law wind, or 18–52 % of calm ISA/dry-adiabatic GA300 samples. Source-end grazing is something else. The aircraft sits inside the downward-refracting layer, and the sound arrives on a ray that turns above it. Under the calm inversion that is 100 % of the "lost" samples, and 55–100 % under either LLJ **[SIM]**. Those samples are audible, so the silence term must not treat them as an aircraft that should have been heard and wasn't. The Doppler bias of the turning branch is **untested**, and it is the main open physics item for low GA at night. The old `raytrace_bias.py` also had two artefacts. Constant-c layers let rays duct along a single 1 m layer to 22,000 km, so it never showed the lapse shadow. And its column stopped at H − 0.5 m, which explains the −1 ms "excess delay" printed for ISA rows. Neither artefact changes DF's temperature conclusion.

The literature behind the atmosphere budget holds with sharper numbers. Fovell and Gallagher, now read in full, put HRRR analysis winds 0.4–0.64 m/s too slow near 280 m at 12 UTC (end of night in CONUS). Day-ahead forecasts run up to +1.1 m/s too fast, and the radiosonde verification floor is itself 2.5–4 m/s ([Fovell & Gallagher 2020](https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.atmos.albany.edu/facstaff/rfovell/NWP/2020-fovell-gallagher.pdf)). The operational rule is therefore to use HRRR analysis hours, not forecasts. ERA5 hub-height RMSE is 1.1–1.35 m/s on flat sites and 3.4–3.7 m/s on mountain sites, with no stability split available ([Gualtieri 2021](https://web.archive.org/web/2023id_/https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/14/4169/htm)). That makes terrain class the usable prior for σ_w. Pieren and Lincke's coherence-loss result holds, but their validation was weak morning turbulence. Fringes dissolve at 250 Hz–2 kHz at long range and **stay intact at low frequency and short range** ([JASA 2022](https://www.dora.lib4ri.ch/empa/dload/empa:28783/PDF/view)). Below 250 Hz near CPA, `sim`'s coherent ground mirror is therefore the right model, and coherence loss belongs only to higher harmonics at range. On the uniform-medium sim on main, a 10 K temperature error costs 35–95 m on two-event cases and is recovered by a shared c profile in 6/6 cases. A 6 m/s uniform wind is about 10 m and cannot be resolved at n = 6 **[MEASURED-dp-core]** ([roadmap_experiments_2026-11-03.md](roadmap_experiments_2026-11-03.md)). That is consistent with the ray trace: uniform wind is nearly harmless, and shear is the term that bites.

### Estimator and pipeline biases measured in dp-core

The measurement harness lives in a separate worktree, `/srv/agents/work/doppler_print-measure`, on branch `measure/estimator-bias` (commit `03136b9`, unpushed). It consists of `#[ignore]` tests in `crates/dp-core/src/events/measure/*.rs`, run through `tools/measure.sh <module::test>`, which uses a per-worktree `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` with `-j4 --release`. Its only changes to production code are visibility changes (`peak_near` and `discover::confident` become `pub(super)`, `eval::run_case` becomes `pub`) plus a test-only `candidate::PLATEAU_FIX` ablation flag ([pipeline_bias_measurements.md](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization verification/pipeline_bias_measurements.md>)).

**M1: the ridge-frequency bias has two causes, and neither is picket-fence.** A Candan three-sample estimator on the complex Hann DFT gives ≤ 0.004–0.008 bins RMS at 40 dB. The production `FrontEnd` image gives 0.6–0.97 bins. The first cause is the flat-top tie-break at [candidate.rs:80](../../crates/dp-core/src/events/candidate.rs). `max_by` returns the last of several equal maxima, and a line clipped at +8 z by [whiten.rs:76](../../crates/dp-core/src/audio/whiten.rs) has a flat top, so `den = 0` and δ = 0. Strong lines therefore read **+0.5 to +0.8 bins high** (0.36–0.58 % of f). The second cause is the 60 s quantile floor. On slow ridges (α ≤ 0.05 Hz/s, the asymptotes of far or slow passes) a strong line occupies one bin long enough to be partly subtracted as background. That adds **0.4–0.9 bins of error with ρ1 ≈ 0.97**, against 0.09 bins on the raw-dB image. The second cause is what drives the fit bias. Fed with the production ridge, the free CV fit overestimates **d by +2 to +40 % and v by +0.5 to +15 %, and puts t_cpa late by up to +4.2 s**. The bias grows with SNR and with d/v, which is the opposite of noise behaviour. Fixing the plateau alone leaves the fit rows unchanged. Re-reading the ridge on raw dB removes about 95 % of the bias, and Candan removes all of it (|d| ≤ 0.3 %, |t_cpa| ≤ 0.07 s) **[MEASURED-dp-core]**. Today this bias reaches the search only through `EventSpec` pruning bounds (d ∈ [d/2, 2d], v ± 20 %), so a +40 % d still fits inside them. It does hit everything that trusts fitted values: association ranking, reported d and v, a drift term in the event fit, and MM's planned CPA-locus seeds and EDT score. MM's 8–20 m locus prototype assumed σ_t = 0.1 s and 2 % slant error, whereas the measured biases are up to 4 s and 40 %. The minimal fix has three parts. Run `peak_near` on one window's unwhitened log power in `candidate.rs::analyze` (the function starts at :123). Better, add `audio::refine_if` (Candan for Hann, 2–3 iterations; port `events/measure/candan.rs`) and call it from `discover::finalize` before `fit_cv_with` (discover.rs:127–132 on main). Separately, return the centre of a flat-top run at candidate.rs:80–87.

**M2: drift.** This is covered in the f0 drift section below. **M3: codecs through the real pipeline.** With oracle events, error and clock were bit-identical across WAV, AAC, MP3, Opus and AAC→Opus on the two sim scenarios that localise (19 m and 78 m). `faint_single` is degenerate along track under every variant, WAV included. Discovery is the fragile stage. Recall and event counts flip under perturbations as small as MP3 128k, codecs add 3–4 spurious events on `two_props_920s`, and the **discovered-event search failed or missed by about 5 km in 10 of 12 runs** **[MEASURED-dp-core]**. The run also exposed two decoder defects in [audio/decode.rs](../../crates/dp-core/src/audio/decode.rs). symphonia has no Opus decoder, so `.opus` and `.webm` (YouTube itag 251) fail outright. And it does not trim AAC priming in m4a, leaving a fixed 1024-sample (21.3 ms at 48 kHz) lag despite `enable_gapless` at :41. On one turning scenario, symphonia-decoded AAC also lost an event that the ffmpeg-decoded copy of the same file kept (n = 1).

**Which results still hold on main.** The measurements were taken at `6d84554`, and main is now at `9641802`. Another session is actively editing search code, and the working tree has uncommitted changes. `git diff 6d84554 HEAD` shows `events/candidate.rs`, `events/fit.rs`, `audio/` and `sim/` unchanged, and the file:line references above were re-checked against main. So the **M1 picket results, the M1/M2 free-fit biases and the M3 decoder findings stand**. Discovery did change: `stitch` moved out of `merge.rs` into a new `events/stitch.rs` (+153/−99 lines) and `discover.rs` changed its import. The production-ridge fit rows depend on discovery and are cheap to confirm (`fitbias::cv_fit_bias`, about 4 min). The drift-in-search figures (150 m / 700–900 m) are therefore indicative until they are re-run against current scoring.

### f0 drift: larger than predicted, opposite in sign, absent from the search

AF §3's first-order rule, d bias ≈ β·c·d/v², is refuted in both directions. In a free fit, rising f0 shrinks the logarithmic frequency span, so v and d are both biased **low**. At 1 %/min that is v −4.7 % / −6.7 % and d −7.7 % / −13.3 % for 1 km at 50 m/s over ±30 / ±60 s, and d −54 % to −56 % for a 30 m/s helicopter at 5 km **[derived]** ([derived_checks.md](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization verification/derived_checks.md>)). With v fixed from ADS-B, d is biased high by +6 to +51 %, and because σd/d is then only 1–9 %, that is **4–16σ**. dp-core agrees on magnitude and asymmetry. At 1 %/min the change in d is 2–15× the formula, falling rpm hurts more (up to +54 % d at 3 km and 50 m/s), and **0.25 %/min already moves d by −2 to −11 %** **[MEASURED-dp-core]**. The new finding is that the search has no β term. With oracle events and a 1 %/min drift, the cross-track locate error rose from about 20 m to **−155 / +142 m at 1.5 km and −895 / +686 m at 5 km**. That is 10–18 % of d, and of the same size as the jet-at-2-km atmosphere terms that headed AF's ranking. The closed form exists, but it is exactly linear in f, not ln f: f = f0·D(t) + (f0β)·τ·D(t) recovers β exactly. MM already showed the ln-f version differs only by (βτ)²/2, below 1 % of per-frame σ under 3 %/min, so either form serves the power path. Identifiability needs a span of at least ±(2–3)·d/v. Freeing β on a ±1 d/v span inflates cross-track σ 3.6×, which is a principled reason for class priors on β (σ_β = 5e-5 to 2e-4 /s) rather than a free parameter. Drift should enter the event fit only after the M1 fix. Until then, the image bias (+15–40 % d) is as large as the drift bias it would be estimating.

### Mirror and along-track: what actually breaks each

The along-track/clock degeneracy is exact, and wider than stated. It survives uniform wind in any direction, and in a constant-rate turn it persists as a rotation about the turn centre, (φ, δ + φ/ω), with a residual of 3e-14 Hz **[derived]**. AF's "a turn or a second event breaks it" needs restating: only *changing* curvature (a turn entry at CPA gives σx 22 m, σδ 0.39 s) or a non-parallel second pass breaks it, and σx grows as 1/sin Δheading (29 / 77 / 306 m at 60 / 20 / 5°). The clock-grid rule changes too. A step equal to Δs/v is the worst choice, because the grid lattice and the along-track lattice then line up and the best node can sit Δδ/2 = 1.43 s off. That costs Δχ² ≈ 450 at σf = 0.39 Hz. The real criterion is Δδ/2 ≤ timing tolerance: 0.48–0.96 s for curve-sum scoring and **0.07 s for a likelihood** **[derived]**. MM's local Laplace or quadrature over δ satisfies it.

The mirror result is the sharpest correction. Argentiero and Marini's numbers are confirmed in full text: their most powerful test is right 67 % of the time at 10 Hz resolution and 99.99 % at 1 Hz ([TM-78049 pp. 19–20](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19780011233/downloads/19780011233.pdf)). But that asymmetry comes from Earth rotation acting as track curvature. For doppler_print's straight pass, **P(correct mirror) = 0.5 exactly**, at every σ, span, altitude profile, uniform wind, DEM and c error. The Doppler curve depends only on perpendicular distance to the 3-D track line, so the crosswind and DEM-height "breakers" in the identifiability note are refuted **[derived]**. Constant turns separate the mirrors only conditionally. The circle inversion ρ′ = (r² + H²)/ρ is an exact twin once c′/c = √(r² + H²)/ρ, which is 0.35–3.7 % for gentle GA turns. So a constant turn resolves the mirror only as well as c is known, and a +3 % c error in the model makes the decision confidently wrong (P = 0.00–0.02 for GA300 at 0.3°/s). Turn entries and second passes are robust. A 3 % c error moves |r_true| by only 0.5–12 Hz·√frame against |r_mirror| of 6–94, and unmodelled 5 m/s wind never flipped a case. The full σ90 table and the four "always report both modes" rules (straight audible window; a turn whose c′/c − 1 < 3σ_c; σ_frame > 0.55·σ90; a one-sided curve with y ≤ 1 km) are in derived_checks. Two caveats carry over: frames were assumed independent, and wind shear, the one physical breaker for straight passes, was not tested and is quantifiable with `mmray.py`.

On non-Doppler breakers, the stereo-bearing figures are confirmed (7.5° / 2.5° / 0.76° at 100 / 300 / 1000 Hz). The claim that stereo "adds much to truncated passes" holds only when the mic-axis azimuth is known (×2–4 in σd). With an unknown phone heading it falls to ×1.0–1.3, and stereo becomes a handedness bit and nothing more. The corpus cannot test even that: its only two-channel file is dual mono **[MEASURED-corpus]**. On main's Livermore baseline, truth survives catalogue, nearby-flight and association stages in 28/28 dev cases, and the median score gap between best and truth cell is 0.59 z ([roadmap_experiments_2026-11-03.md](roadmap_experiments_2026-11-03.md)). The real-data errors are flat mirror and along-track ridges, not search misses, which is why mode reporting moves up the roadmap.

### Codecs and the recording chain: frequencies survive, amplitude and stereo do not

Controlled synthetic runs through ffmpeg 7 cover 12 chains, 4 maskers and 7 tone-to-masker ratios in mono, stereo and quiet conditions ([codec_measurements.md](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization verification/codec_measurements.md>), raw tables in `codec_results/exp{1..5}_summary.md`). They settle AF's open codec question for propeller tones. **No chain added ≥ 10 % spectral holes to 88–704 Hz harmonics at any local TMR down to −21 dB/ERB, and the codec frequency bias is ≤ 0.008 Hz in every cell** **[MEASURED-synthetic]**. The right treatment is to inflate per-frame σ in quadrature (+0.03 Hz at TMR ≥ 0, +0.3 Hz below, for 0.25 s windows), not to correct a bias. Deletions are specific. Fan tones above 1 kHz under wind lose 0.7–3 dB in mono Opus and 10–13 dB in stereo Opus at every TMR. AAC-LC stereo under a speech-shaped masker makes holes below −7 dB/ERB. The variable that predicts a hole is the tone's level relative to the loudest bands in the same frame, not ERB-local TMR. AAC-LC also emits short spurious lines at 1.5–3.7 kHz, about 1.7/s on average and up to 39/s, so tracks seeded above 1 kHz in AAC need ≥ 0.5 s continuity. Stream choice follows. For mono prop and rotor work, itags 140 and 251 are equivalent. Prefer 140 (AAC-LC 128) when stereo or > 1 kHz fan tones matter, because 251 is AAC→Opus and Opus collapses low-frequency inter-channel delay (slope 0.63–0.91 below 300 Hz, worse for louder tones). Above 600 Hz every chain keeps the delay within ±6 %. HE-AAC (itag 139) was not measured: no SBR encoder was available.

Noise suppression behaves differently from DF's assumption. WebRTC-style NS treats a steady tone as noise and pulls it down within seconds to the same depth as the noise (−6 to −25 dB by level). TNR is preserved and the bias is ≤ 0.001 Hz, so NS **invalidates amplitude-versus-range use but not frequency tracks**. Spectral subtraction does erode weak 1–2 kHz tones by up to 20 dB **[MEASURED-synthetic]**. The genuine deafness mechanism in the corpus is high-pass filtering. Eight of 40 Livermore files have a 40–160 Hz corner, which removes most piston and turboprop blade-pass fundamentals, and the 20–80 Hz wind detector is blind in exactly those files **[MEASURED-corpus]**. ENF accuracy is codec-independent through AAC, Opus and AAC→Opus, extending Archer's MP3 result ([Archer 2011](https://www.ucdenver.edu/docs/librariesprovider27/ncmf-docs/theses/archer_thesis_fall2011.pdf)). Success depends only on hum SNR and clip length: 0 % at 0 dB/Hz, and at 5 dB/Hz 53–67 % at 2 min and 100 % at 5 min **[MEASURED-synthetic]**. That matches Vatansever's 0–26 % at 1 min ([poster](https://sigport.org/sites/default/files/docs/ICASSP_Poster_v6.pdf)). The rule is: require ≥ 2 min and per-line SNR ≥ 15 dB before trusting an absolute time.

### What the real corpus contains

The 175 audio files dedupe to **64 unique recordings: 40 Livermore smartphone passes, 1 Oyster Bay hour, 16 NASA UAV flyovers and 7 DOPPLERGEIST synthetics** ([corpus_signatures.md](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization verification/corpus_signatures.md>)). The Livermore files are bare `fmt`+`data` WAV exports with no tags, and the Oyster Bay file is a yt-dlp decode of itag 251. No file is a phone video upload, so every cue that depends on MP4 metadata, orientation tags or AAC upload artefacts is unmeasured on real data. None of the 57 real files carries an absolute-time anchor. The project does, however, get one free clock. Aligning each published Livermore ridge to its audio places sample zero at **window start + 5.00 s (±0.07 s) in 32/40 cases**. That is about 0.05 s of clock truth that `truth.json` lacks. Because main's `eval/livermore.rs` uses the same ridge-alignment method, the regularity serves as a consistency check on it rather than an independent clock. The 8 outliers (dt0 0.34–60.2 s) need a manual look. Oyster Bay's content stops at a 9.47 kHz shelf below the Opus 20 kHz edge, which points to an unknown upstream lossy stage.

The tonal picture argues against several earlier assumptions. Hum is US 60 Hz, carried by even harmonics, and the fundamental is almost never present. Oyster Bay shows coherent grid wander on its 360 Hz and 240 Hz lines (1-min means agree to ≤ 3 mHz), but no Eastern Interconnection reference for 2026-09-08 is held. **Persistent non-mains lines are common and sit squarely in the blade-pass band**: a 58.25 Hz motor-slip line in 13/40 Livermore files, an unexplained 25 Hz line in 15/40, and 101, 137 and 987 Hz lines in Oyster Bay. A harmonic tracker seeded at 112.4 Hz locked onto the 120 Hz hum. Published Livermore ridges are overtones (h ≥ 2) in at least 33/40 cases, and the Oyster Bay B350 event carries a 0.5× line as strong as k1. Distant real events show only k1 and k2 above the noise null. Ridges fragment into about 4 runs per pass with a median longest gap of 0.45 s (p90 0.78 s), so 1–2 s gap bridging covers every measured ridge. Lengthening the window from 0.256 s to 1.02 s gains 4–6 dB on distant ridges **[MEASURED-corpus]**. The synthetic fixtures run the other way: a 29 dB 60 Hz fundamental hum with single-harmonic tones. `sim`'s chain model therefore needs the real pattern before it can stand in for the corpus.

### Literature numbers that changed

Several figures carried through the earlier reports change on full-text reading. Mode-S EHS temperature error attributed to Mirza is 1.25–2.5 K, not 4–7 K; the 4–7 K figure may come from KNMI TR-338, which was not checked ([Mirza et al.](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/65960/1/MirzaEtAl_AuthorFinalVersion.pdf)). Doweck's harmonic-chirp estimator is EUSIPCO 2015 and reaches the CRLB only at ≥ 7 dB total-harmonic SNR ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/54339/files/1570087825.pdf)). DeepLofargram's −24 dB is a time-domain per-sample SNR, with line-location accuracy of only 0.28–0.45 ([arXiv 1912.00605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00605)). The Säilynoja citation for ECDF-band SBC plots is arXiv 2103.10522 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10522)). The Volpe 2010 source-noise report supplies per-type 1000 ft LASmax priors: light pistons and the S300C at about 64–66 dBA, the C208B, Bell 407 and R44 at about 70–72 dBA ([Volpe dot/6451](https://web.archive.org/web/2021id_/https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/6451/dot_6451_DS1.pdf)). CR-189705 is an analytic design study, not a measurement. Its ~14 dB per distance doubling at grazing angles is the worst-case slope the P_d model should carry for low-elevation tracks ([NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19950021652/downloads/19950021652.pdf)). Real-flight accuracy figures for single-sensor aircraft Doppler (Ferguson 1992, Ferguson & Quinn 1994, Lo & Ferguson) remain behind closed access. No published number yet anchors doppler_print's real-data error.

Three moves matter most. The front-end fix and the drift term (#1, #3) jump ahead of atmosphere because they are measured inside dp-core, larger than or comparable to the atmospheric terms, and cheap. Discovery robustness and mode reporting (#4, #5) jump because both the measured runs and main's Livermore baseline fail there, not at propagation. Stereo, region masks and the elevation gate drop because the corpus cannot validate them or they were measured to misfire.

## Conclusion

The earlier reports treated dp-core's signal path as a near-ideal measuring instrument and looked outward, to the atmosphere, ADS-B and recording devices, for the error budget. Measuring the instrument itself reversed that. The largest systematic error found this round is in-house and fixable in an afternoon, and the largest source-side error (drift) is absent from the search entirely. Most external effects either check out as predicted (temperature and wind with the right averaging) or turn out smaller than feared (codecs). The degeneracies, meanwhile, are harder than assumed: no amount of Doppler precision resolves a straight-pass mirror, and the one correction that sounded free (finer resolution) does nothing.

The methodological lesson matches thunderprint's. Every surprise this round came from running the real code or the real audio: the tie-break bias, the decoder lag, the 3/57 false rate verdicts, the overtone ridges, the persistent motor line. None came from derivations that shared the model's assumptions, and the one derived result that did change the physics (the circle-inversion twin) did so because it was checked against an independent forward model. The roadmap therefore keeps a measurement harness next to the pipeline and makes `sim` able to disagree with `physics`. No atmospheric or silence default should be promoted until a stratified, turning-ray `sim` and the 41 real cases have been given the chance to reject it.
