# Wind, silence and clocks now bound accuracy

**Beyond what the first accuracy round established, the most useful additions to doppler_print are not new physics. They are information that removes specific ambiguities. Wind aloft is the main remaining atmospheric bias, the silence term needs a real audibility model, and an independent clock anchor is the only thing that breaks the along-track degeneracy.** The new notes change the earlier ranking in three ways. First, temperature and wind should be split: any routine source gets path-mean temperature to about ±2 K, which is already below the wind error budget, while nocturnal low-level winds stay uncertain by 2–3 m/s even in HRRR and ERA5 ([NOAA 74031](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/74031)). Second, a curved-ray check run against thunderprint's refraction model shows that refraction barely biases the Doppler fit once a chord-averaged profile is used: 8 m or less for GA, 25 m or less for jets **[derived]**. It does, however, create shadow zones 3–23 km out that the silence term currently penalises as if the aircraft should have been heard. Third, a structural result organises everything else. Under constant velocity, the along-track/clock shift leaves the whole receiver-to-aircraft vector unchanged **[derived]**, so no stereo bearing, video angle or visual-acoustic lag can break it. Only absolute time (ENF, sirens, bells, lightning, train logs), a turn or second event, or scene geolocation can. The mirror ambiguity, by contrast, falls to any single reliable bit of handedness: the direction the aircraft crosses the video frame, the sign of a stereo sweep, or water on one side of the track. Per-type tone priors, an explicit unknown-aircraft hypothesis and ML clutter masks mainly address association errors, and are worth roughly 2 nats per candidate at best **[derived]**. Two cautions apply. The real corpus has nine cases, and with n = 9 any hit-rate estimate carries a ±0.3 confidence interval **[derived]**, so almost every claim here must be tested in `sim` first. And `sim` currently uses a uniform medium, so it cannot falsify the atmosphere model it is meant to check. Evidence quality is uneven. The session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through several research threads, so many figures below come from abstracts or search snippets, and most rows in the per-type tone tables are estimates. Numbers tagged **[derived]** or **[SIM]** come from the researchers' own arithmetic and scripts (in [`scripts/`](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization deeper factors/scripts/>)), not from the literature.

## Each cue removes one of five error classes

The first report identified the error structure: a single pass fixes rest frequency, v/c, CPA slant range and CPA time. That leaves an exact along-track/clock trade-off, a two-fold mirror across the ground track, and a (c, v, d) scale invariance that turns any sound-speed error into a range error. Add the two inference failures the pipeline can make (associating an event with the wrong flight, and penalising the true cell for "missing" an aircraft it could not have heard), and every factor in this round falls into one of five classes. The researchers' key structural derivation is that **the along-track/clock shift (Δs, Δs/v) leaves the receiver-to-aircraft relative vector r(t_audio) unchanged, not just the Doppler curve** **[derived]**. No measurement of relative geometry can therefore break it: not stereo bearing, visual elevation, visual-acoustic lag, or even absolute azimuth from a sun-calibrated camera. The mirror flips only the sign of the cross-track component, so one bit of handedness or absolute azimuth is enough to resolve it.

| Error class | What breaks or reduces it | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror ambiguity | Video traverse direction; stereo L→R sweep sign; sun/shadow camera heading; water or terrain on one side; tempered population prior; skyline–DEM fix | One bit suffices; video is codec-proof |
| Along-track/clock degeneracy | ENF (after a coarse prior); siren tests and radio-synced bells; lightning strike + thunder; train TD/TRUST logs; FRA horn + crossing (clock-free offset); launch booms; skyline–DEM; turns/second event | Only absolute time or scene position works |
| Sound-speed/wind bias | HRRR/ERA5 profiles; METAR + lapse by day; GNSS-minus-baro fleet temperature; EHS winds near TMAs; multi-event reciprocal headings; visible-aircraft elevation (breaks c scale) | Temperature easy, wind hard |
| Association errors | Type tone priors and Doppler-invariant ratios (rotor TR/MR, jet blade count, piston firing/BPF); unknown-aircraft/clutter hypotheses; ML clutter masks; ENF-bin exclusion; APU rejection | ≤ ~2 nats per candidate **[derived]** |
| False silence penalties | P_d from measured floor along predicted curve; shadow-zone edge √(2RH); DEM line of sight; wind/noise-suppression "deaf" state; MLAT/FLARM ingestion; unknown-aircraft cap | Prevents vetoing the true cell |

Region-level cues (mains frequency 50/60 Hz, traction 16.7/25 Hz, GSM buzz, bird species, language, rain) do not fall cleanly into any one class. They act as masks on the posterior that restrict which reanalysis profile and which ADS-B day apply. Static acoustic sources (wind turbines, roads, transformer hum) add distance masks but cannot break any Doppler degeneracy, because they do not move.

## Wind, not temperature, carries the atmospheric bias

The earlier report ranked a path-mean sound speed c̄ and an along-track wind w̄ together as its top change. Its simulated table did support that: a fixed 343 m/s on a 0 °C day (≈20 K error) costs 69 m for low GA and 801 m for a jet at 2 km **[SIM]**. The new atmospheric notes show those two halves should be split, because they cost very different amounts to fix. **[derived]** dc/dT ≈ 0.59 m/s per K, so 1 K of column error is about 0.17% of c, or roughly 1.7 m of range per km of slant. A 10 m/s along-track wind error is about 3%. Scaling the earlier table linearly, a ±2 K temperature error is worth roughly 7 m (GA) to 50 m (jets) **[derived]**. Any routine source already reaches that level: METAR plus a 6.5 K/km lapse in daytime mixed conditions, ERA5/HRRR at any hour, or the hypsometric fleet estimate below. Wind is harder. Both ERA5 and HRRR **underestimate low-level jet speeds and misplace boundary-layer thermal structure** offshore of the US East Coast ([NOAA 74031](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/74031)). ERA5 underestimates strong North Sea LLJs ([WES 2026](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/2307/2026/)) and **overestimates night-time desert winds by about 2 m/s** ([MDPI RS 17:393](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-17-00393/article_deploy/remotesensing-17-00393.pdf)). The residual wind error at night is therefore 2–3 m/s, concentrated in the lowest few hundred metres, which is the layer that dominates the path average for low GA. Once both inputs are sourced, the residual biases for jets are comparable. What makes wind the priority is that only it stays uncertain after sourcing. Temperature to ±2 K is nearly free; wind to ±1 m/s at night is not available from any public product.

Three temperature sources were examined, and none needs to be built before the wind path. The cheapest is the **GNSS-minus-baro hypsometric difference**, which the first report proposed but had not tested. It works only after the QNH term is removed. That term is about 8 m/hPa and swamps the column signal: ±20 hPa gives ±160 m, while a 10 K anomaly at 1 km gives only ±35 m **[derived]**. With METAR QNH as the datum (never the crew-selected `nav_qnh`), the residual is the mean anomaly of the layer from the station to the aircraft. The sensitivity is only 3–3.5 m per K at 1 km **[derived]**, so 25 ft quantisation and altimetry-system error limit a single low GA aircraft to 5–15 K. Averaging airliners at 1–3 km brings it to 1–3 K. One published retrieval found **1.58 °C mean RMSE against ECMWF**, but over only 11 flights, reported in a regional journal and read at abstract level ([Kalintsev 2024](https://if-mstuca.ru/CE/index.php/transport-systems/s15031-en)). Humidity enters through virtual temperature: **[derived]** it adds up to +1.7 m/s (0.5%) at 25 °C saturated and 3.1 m/s (0.9%) at 35 °C, comparable to a 3–5 K error in summer, and negligible above 2 km or below 10 °C. HRRR is archived on AWS from **2014-07-30** and reachable through Herbie ([Herbie docs](https://herbie.readthedocs.io/en/2024.3.0/user_guide/_model_notebooks/hrrr.html)). ERA5T arrives about **5 days** behind real time, and final ERA5 two to three months later ([ECMWF ERA5](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/spaces/GCR/pages/473842427/ECMWF+ERA5)). Mode-S EHS winds exist mainly for jets near Mode-S radars. EHS-derived temperature has **4–7 K error from Mach resolution alone** ([Mirza et al.](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/65960/1/MirzaEtAl_AuthorFinalVersion.pdf)) and readsb inhibits it below Mach 0.395 ([readsb README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiedehopf/readsb/dev/README-json.md)), so it is useless for GA. Aggregating nearby jet winds meteo-particle-style cut wind MAE by **67% relative to interpolated GFS** ([Sun et al. 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0205029.html)). That is the best observational wind aloft for events near a terminal area. The direct meteorological message (BDS 4,4) is too rare to rely on: **16% of aircraft at Maastricht against under 1% in APAC** ([KNMI IR2014-01](https://cdn.knmi.nl/knmi/pdf/bibliotheek/knmipubIR/IR2014-01.pdf); [ICAO DAPs WG3](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/APAC/Meetings/2020/2020%20DAPs%20WG3/4-Information%20Paper/IP08_CHN%20AI.3%20-%20Aircraft%20Parameters%20Availability%20in%20Mode%20S%20DAPs%20and%20ADS-B%20ADD.pdf)).

The averaging rule is simpler than the earlier report implied. **[derived]** Along a straight or weakly refracted chord from the receiver at z_r to the aircraft at z_a, both c and the along-ray wind enter travel time with **uniform weight in height on [z_r, z_a]**, independent of elevation angle. The harmonic and arithmetic z-means differ by less than 0.02 m/s. That has three consequences. c̄ and w̄ must be recomputed per ADS-B sample, because the window moves with the aircraft altitude. The lower bound is the receiver's DEM height, not sea level. And the lowest 100–300 m, where inversions and shear sit, carries weight Δz/(z_a − z_r), so it dominates for low GA. The researchers propose nuisance priors of σ_c ≈ 0.6 m/s and σ_w ≈ 1.5 m/s by day, and 1.5–2 and 2.5–3 m/s at night below 500 m. These are judgement calls scaled from the LLJ findings, not measurements. One project-rule tension needs resolving here. The atmospheric notes sketch a Python pipeline that computes Tv-based c and z-means. Under `CLAUDE.md`, Python may only fetch and subset GRIB fields; the per-sample z-integration belongs in `dp-core`, next to the existing `[atmosphere]` slot. That placement also fits the requirement that c̄ change along the track.

**The thunderprint-style ray-trace shows refraction does not bias the fit, but it does change audibility.** The researchers ran a Snell-shooting curved-ray truth model in 1 m layers and fitted it with straight chords (`raytrace_bias.py`) **[derived]**. A chord with the true profile integrated along it reproduced the curved-ray Doppler to **≤ 8 m cross-track for GA/approach geometries** (down to 2° elevation and 8 km slant) and to **≤ 6–25 m for jets** at 2–3 km. The worst case was a dry-adiabatic profile with an 8 km slant window. Ignoring the profile entirely costs 4–525 m. Excess ray-minus-chord delays stayed within about 30 ms, as Fermat's principle requires. This confirms, for temperature stratification, the earlier report's claim that a straight chord is correct to first order. It leaves two things open. First, the **wind-shear rows were discarded** because the effective-sound-speed approximation produced positive excess delays that Fermat forbids, so the largest prior bias term (shear) has still not been checked against curved rays. Second, those curved rays produce **shadow zones**: x_max = √(2RH) gives edges at 5–7 km for H = 300 m, 9.5–13 km at 1 km and 16–23 km at 3 km, across ISA, dry-adiabatic and upwind-shear cases **[derived]**. A +10 K inversion over the lowest 300 m left **62–70% of samples within 8 km in geometric shadow** for low GA. The earlier silence model capped P_d at 0.9 and down-weighted elevations below 10–15°, but nothing in it encodes that an aircraft beyond the shadow edge is geometrically inaudible. That gap is addressed in the next section. The same results suggest the earlier elevation gate (rank 11) may discard information without removing any bias for temperature profiles, so it should be re-tested rather than adopted.

## Silence evidence needs audibility, shadows and an unknown-aircraft escape

The silence term is where the new notes add the most structure. The earlier design (∏(1 − P_d) with P_d ≤ 0.9 and a latent "deaf recording" state) is still correct, but P_d itself was a constant. The audibility researchers assembled its components. On source level, **no public database gives tonal source levels per ICAO type**. ANP/AEDT NPDs give A-weighted broadband levels against distance, with a 1/3-octave spectral class from 50 Hz to 10 kHz, and EASA maps unlisted types to proxies ([EASA ANP substitutions](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/environment/policy-support-and-research/aircraft-noise-and-performance-anp-data/anp-aircraft-substitutions)). For helicopters, whose main-rotor tones lie below 50 Hz, that band floor matters ([Volpe dot/12004](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/12004/dot_12004_DS1.pdf)). A per-class tone-to-broadband offset therefore has to be learned from the corpus. On propagation, octave-band absorption is only **0.1 dB/km at 63 Hz and 0.4 dB/km at 125 Hz** but 3.7 dB/km at 1 kHz (10 °C, 70% RH) ([windPRO/ISO 9613](https://help.emd.dk/knowledgebase/content/windPRO4.1/Appendix_A_DECIBEL.pdf)). Overhead GA passes are therefore spreading-limited. Distant low-elevation passes are limited by ground effect and refraction, and a measured helicopter study (seen only as a snippet) reports 5–10 dB/km of excess attenuation ([IOA, Soilleux](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/PJ%20SOILLEUX%20MEASUREMENT%20OF%20ACOUSTIC%20PROPAGATION%20THROUGH%20THE%20ATMOSPHERE.pdf)). On ambient noise, the NPS geospatial L50 model has **1.7 dB (urban) and 3.1 dB (natural) leave-one-out median error** ([NPS FAQ](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/sound/mapfaq.htm)). The researchers' key point is that GIS ambient is mostly irrelevant here. The recording is fixed, so the noise term in P_d is the recording's own whitened noise floor measured in the time window of the predicted curve. GIS ambient only explains why that floor is what it is.

The detection threshold follows from signal-detection theory. The US federal audibility standard computes a d′ per 1/3-octave band. **Attentive listeners heard tour aircraft at D′L ≈ 7 dB, and D′L ≈ 17 dB is used for noticeability by distracted visitors** ([Volpe/Page 2018](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/54900/dot_54900_DS1.pdf)). **[derived]** A machine square-law detector that sums whitened power along an ADS-B-predicted curve over K ≈ 60 independent frames (Δf = 2 Hz) reaches P_d = 0.9 at P_fa = 10⁻³ with a per-bin SNR of about −2.5 dB. That puts it **15–18 dB more sensitive than a human** on a steady tone. The researchers map this to P_d = min(0.9, Φ((SNR_pred − SNR_thr)/σ_tot)) with σ_tot ≈ 6–8 dB, combining source, propagation and ambient uncertainty. A σ that large makes the curve shallow, which is the principled reason the cap and the deaf state matter. P_d should also include the thunderprint shadow edge (P_d → a small floor beyond √(2RH), with lapse rate and shear as nuisances; thunderprint's `shadow_alt_km` is the one-line template) and a DEM line-of-sight test around CPA, which reduces SNR through diffraction rather than zeroing it. Wind is a recording-level deafness mechanism. **[derived]** For a 1 cm phone port, Strasberg's fD/U = 5 scaling puts wind noise up to about 2.5 kHz at a 5 m/s breeze, covering the whole aircraft-tone band. Phone stationary-noise suppressors also specifically remove steady tones such as prop and jet lines. Both argue for a per-frame wind detector that down-weights silence evidence, plus the latent deaf prior. The only end-to-end detection ranges found are heterogeneous. Buried fibre detected **propeller aircraft at 2–3 km** ([EarthDoc 2024](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.202475013)), and a 5–7.5 km figure for an acoustic+ADS-B study could not be verified. The best P_d(range) curve will come from the project's own corpus: for every ADS-B flight around each true location, record whether it was detected.

**Missing traffic is the other half of false silence penalties, and also a source of association errors.** In the US, ADS-B Out is mandatory only in rule airspace. In 2019 the DOT OIG found only **44% (62,494 of 143,322)** of GA aircraft expected to equip had done so ([GA News](https://generalaviationnews.com/2019/09/19/only-44-of-ga-aircraft-equipped-with-ads-b)). A vendor estimate puts **20,000–30,000 unequipped GA aircraft and helicopters** flying low in uncontrolled airspace ([uAvionix](https://uavionix.com/harnessing-the-shifting-landscape-of-ads-b-equipage-opportunities-for-electronic-conspicuity-and-uas/)). The EU mandate covers only aircraft **over 5,700 kg MTOW or faster than 250 kt** ([Olive et al. 2023](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/14442/3052.pdf)), so most European light aircraft, gliders and ultralights are absent. LADD does not bind hobbyist networks. In one measurement **92% of business, 64% of military and 96% of government aircraft** had some public-dissemination block ([PoPETs 2018](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/9909/popets-2018-0023.pdf)), but raw feeds still carry them. Military traffic is often Mode S only and therefore visible through MLAT. OpenSky has archived FLARM since late 2018 in `flarm_raw`, but from only about 30 receivers by 2023 and mostly Swiss and German ([Olive et al. 2023](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/14442/3052.pdf)). OGN-derived glider launches matched **67–92%** of paper records ([SSF 2024](https://soaringsafety.org/presentations/Convention-2024.pdf)). The researchers' working guess that **5–20% of low-altitude prop/helicopter events in US suburbs have no track** (higher in rural Europe) is an inference, to be measured on the corpus. The established fix is Reid's multiple-hypothesis construction: each event comes from a known target, a new (here, undetected) target, or clutter ([Reid 1979](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1102177)). Under Poisson assumptions, the "new target" density should be read as the undetected-target intensity ([Brekke 2018](https://arl.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/publications/Brekke2018_FiniteSetStatistics.pdf)). For doppler_print, each cell's event likelihood becomes a mixture over known flights, λ_u(x)·ℓ_u(e) and λ_c·ℓ_c(e). Here λ_u is built from the ADS-B store's own low-altitude flight-hour density times an "invisibility multiplier" that is larger near gliding fields and in receiver-sparse areas, and ℓ_u integrates the free CPA fit over class priors. Capping each event's penalty at the unknown hypothesis means one unlisted helicopter cannot sink the true cell. The summary should flag a recording as inconclusive when most of its events come out "unknown". The coverage map that weights the silence stage should also feed λ_u. The location prior should not down-weight rural cells as well, because that would penalise them twice.

## Type signatures help association, as tempered ranking evidence

The type researchers built three tables: [`type_tones_props.csv`](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization deeper factors/type_tones_props.csv>), [`type_tones_jets.csv`](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization deeper factors/type_tones_jets.csv>) and [`type_tones_helis.csv`](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization deeper factors/type_tones_helis.csv>). Their coverage and confidence must be read before any row is used. The props table has **58 rows: 3 high, 15 medium and 40 low confidence**. Most piston rpm values are typical POH figures that could not be checked against a TCDS before the search quota ran out, and the note lists the TCDSs to read. The jets table has **35 rows: 5 high, 12 medium and 18 low**. Fan blade counts and 100% N1 are TCDS-grade for the A320ceo/neo and 737NG/MAX engines. %N1 by flight phase is entirely estimated, because no public per-type N1 schedule exists, and several widebody and regional blade counts are blank. The helicopter table has **25 rows**. Main-rotor blade count and rpm are mostly read from EASA/UK CAA TCDSs, but **tail-rotor rpm is the weakest column**: mostly low confidence, from memory or derived from POH tip speeds. The CSVs are generated by scripts (`gen_type_tones_props.py`), so BPF values are arithmetic on these inputs and inherit their uncertainty.

The sourced anchors are solid. The C208 Caravan turns its 3-blade prop at **1900 rpm in every phase, so BPF = 95.0 Hz** ([EASA TCDS IM.A.226](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/7230/en)). A King Air 350 with 5-blade MT props at 1500 rpm produced **measured ground tones at 125, 250 and 375 Hz** ([Grande et al. 2023](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2023/data/articles/000329.pdf)). The CFM56-5B reaches **100% N1 at 5000 rpm** ([EASA E.003](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/7797/en)), LEAP-1A at 3856 rpm ([EASA E.110](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/20086/en)), and the PW1100G fan is geared **1:3.0625** to its LP spool ([UK CAA TCDS](https://www.caa.co.uk/Documents/Download/3939/984aa08e-5352-4da9-824b-309ec7cec37d/3653)). The CF34 (CRJ/E-jet) runs **100% N1 = 7400 rpm** ([EASA IM.E.053](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/18525/en)). The R44 runs at 400 rpm nominal ([EASA IM.R.121](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/7929/en)), and Volpe's measured 13.6 Hz "Helicopter B" implies operators fly it at about 102% ([Volpe dot/12004](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/12004/dot_12004_DS1.pdf)). The broad picture is that GA piston BPFs fall at 57–135 Hz, turboprops at 60–175 Hz and civil helicopter main rotors at 13–33 Hz. Bands overlap heavily, especially around 95–120 Hz. BPF alone therefore does not identify type.

What does identify type are **Doppler-invariant ratios between lines from the same rotating source**. Every harmonic of one source shares the same instantaneous Doppler factor, so ratios survive both Doppler and rpm drift **[derived]**. For helicopters, the tail-to-main rotor ratio separates families: 2-blade teetering light types at about 6–6.6, conventional-tail medium twins at 2.4–4.9, Fenestrons at 22–37, NOTAR with no tail line, and tandems at 1. Within a family, ±2–7% rotor-speed bands blur individual types. A Rafael patent treats the main-rotor fundamental and this ratio as enough to identify a type uniquely ([US 6307810](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6307810)), but reports no accuracy. For jets, **B = BPF / shaft-order spacing is an integer** that separates the 36/24/22/20/18 blade counts of the CFM56-5B, CFM56-7B, V2500, PW1100G and LEAP **[derived]**. On fan BPF alone, by contrast, the CFM56-7B and V2500 approach bands (≈1035–1347 Hz) are numerically identical. For direct-drive pistons, firing/BPF = cyl/(2B), so geared Rotax (1.62) and Austro (1.127) installations show two incommensurate combs, a type fingerprint **[derived]**. Range limits which of these features can be seen. **[derived]** Using ISO 9613-1 absorption, a 2 kHz tone loses about 44 dB over 5 km against about 1 dB at 100 Hz, so fan BPF is a near-field cue (≲2–3 km for CFM56/CF34). Beyond that only shaft and buzz-saw orders at 10–125 Hz survive. They are strong mainly in departure and climb. This also bears on DOPPLERGEIST: its faint CRJ9 event was more likely carried by CF34 shaft orders at 68–123 Hz than by fan BPF.

The literature on acoustic type identification is small and closed-set. One study reports **100% accuracy on known types, collapsing to 65% once unknown types are included** ([ICISD 2025](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/icisd-25/126017024)). doppler_print never needs open-set classification, though. It only needs p(tones | candidate's ADS-B type). The researchers propose a hierarchical log-f0 model (class → type → registration, with flight phase as a covariate), a contamination term ε for wrong type codes, and a mixture over harmonic index k ∈ {½, 1, 2, 3}. The analytic tables would serve as hyperpriors and corpus detections as data. **[derived]** With σ_eff = 0.1 in log, ε = 0.2 and a 20–200 Hz band, the term is bounded at **+2.0 nats for a matching type and −1.6 nats for a totally wrong one**. That is enough to break ties between geometrically similar candidates of different classes (likelihood ratio 7–37×), worth about nothing within one class, and never enough to override a good curve fit. It should start tempered at w = 0.5, built leave-one-registration-out, and reported with and without the term. For US aircraft, the FAA registry gives the engine model per registration but no propeller field, so blade-count retrofits must be learned from per-registration offsets: a consistent 1.5× or 0.67× shift signals a 2↔3-blade change. This complements the earlier report's identifiability result that equal Doppler histories require equal rest tonals ([arXiv 2507.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14765v1)).

### Learned detectors belong upstream of the physics fit

The ML evidence points to one placement. Clip-level aircraft detection is nearly solved under matched conditions: on AeroSonicDB, a CNN scores **99.14% mAP in cross-validation but 91.32% on real urban hours** ([arXiv 2311.06368](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.06368)). None of these classifiers outputs a frequency track. Music and speech F0 nets (CREPE, RMVPE, PESTO) assume one dominant pitch and were tested only down to 0 dB broadband SNR. The closest analogues are marine-mammal whistle extractors, which feed a per-pixel confidence map to a classical tracker and gain **+0.158 F1 (about 25% relative)** ([Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.08894)). The cautionary result comes from continuous gravitational waves: a CNN lost **5–66% sensitivity depth against a coherent matched filter** ([Dreissigacker et al. 2019](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/1904.13291)). doppler_print's ADS-B-predicted curve sum is exactly such a matched template. Neural speech enhancers are documented to drop weak harmonics at low SNR ([Song & Madhu, ICASSP 2023](https://rc.signalprocessingsociety.org/conferences/icassp-2023/spsicassp23vid2554)), and aircraft tones are what speech enhancers are trained to remove. The defensible uses, in increasing order of risk, are: an aircraft-presence prior that re-ranks windows but never vetoes them (the faint CRJ9 is the counterexample to hard gating); a clutter mask for speech, music and road harmonics that weights time-frequency cells; and a sim-trained U-Net that augments the comb image in `events/comb.rs` while frequencies are still read from the unprocessed whitened STFT. Under the project rules, training is offline tooling, and inference must run in `dp-core` through an ONNX runtime such as tract (pure Rust, abi3-friendly), with a parity test against the Python export. Calibrated scores should enter as a feature combined with the comb statistic, not as a probability multiplied into the likelihood. That avoids counting the same evidence twice.

## Independent anchors break the along-track and mirror degeneracies

### Stereo and video give the mirror bit, rarely precision

A phone's two microphones are about **13 cm apart** on a Pixel ([PMC7395928](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7395928)). **[derived]** At 100 Hz the maximum inter-channel phase swing is only ±13.6°, so bearing precision is about 7.6° at 30 dB integrated SNR. It improves to about 0.8° at 1 kHz, which favours coherently combined higher blade-pass harmonics over GCC-PHAT. Cross-track error from bearing is r·σθ ≈ 130 m at 1.5 km, worse than the two-sided Doppler bound of about 20 m. Stereo therefore adds little precision except on truncated or receding-only events, whose (f0, v, d) ridge is nearly collinear and where bearing information is roughly orthogonal to it. That matches thunderprint's measurement that stereo DOA **"added only a few nats at the truth"** ([TP config.rs](../../reference/thunderprint/src/config.rs)). The payoff is the mirror. A pair measures cosθ(t), and the axis alone is mirror-symmetric, but **the sign of d(cosθ)/dt across CPA, given a rear camera and the container's orientation tag, picks the side** **[derived]**. It needs only σθ ≲ 20–30°. Portrait recordings put the mic axis vertical, which measures elevation instead. That is mirror-symmetric, but h/tan ε at CPA gives a ground distance independent of c, which breaks the (c, v, d) scale invariance. The practical risk is fake stereo. iOS exposes built-in stereo as a processed "stereo polar pattern" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/capturing-stereo-audio-from-built-in-microphones.md)), and Opus downmixes to mono at **≤19 kb/s** ([Xiph](https://wiki.xiph.org/Opus_Recommended_Settings)). Inter-channel coherence and lag should be checked per band before phase is trusted. Video is stronger and immune to codecs. The aircraft's horizontal image velocity at CPA is the same handedness bit, robust to roll of up to ±60° and cheap enough to be a human label in the eval UI. Sun-position camera calibration reaches **3° in azimuth** on webcam sequences ([Lalonde et al.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ILIM/projects/IL/sky/skyindex.html)). That fixes absolute azimuth, but as a clock prior it is weak. **[derived]** ±3° of sun azimuth is ±10–15 min, about 50 km along track at 70 m/s. Skyline matching against a DEM is the one video cue that fixes position outright. It has been demonstrated at **country scale over 40,000 km² of Switzerland** ([Baatz et al. ECCV 2012](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2012/baatz2012eccv-large)), and it uses the DEM doppler_print already loads, but it only works in hilly scenes with a horizon in frame.

### Electrical signatures give region, era and sometimes time

The mains-hum (ENF) literature adds one operational rule. For short clips the failure mode is **picking the wrong correlation peak, not imprecision**, and test-recording length is the dominant factor ([Hua et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00176)). The risk grows with the size of the search database, so ENF should run *after* a coarse ADS-B or upload-time prior has narrowed the window. An open 1 s multi-grid reference covers **17 locations in 12 synchronous areas** ([Rydin Gorjão et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02481)). Outdoors, hum is mostly acoustic from transformers at 2× mains, so the 100/120 Hz line is probably the dominant ENF carrier (an inference). Its flat ±0.05 Hz wander must be excluded from Doppler scoring, because it shares the 60–250 Hz band with piston blade-pass lines. Light flicker at 2× mains gives a night-time video ENF route, through rolling-shutter row sampling ([Su et al. ICIP 2014](https://www.mast.umd.edu/files/paper_ENF/conf_paper11_ICIP14.pdf)). A 50 vs 60 Hz label alone is a free hard mask (east vs west Japan, Brazil vs Paraguay). **Railway traction lines** are a second region cue. 16.7 Hz AC is used in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Sweden and Norway ([Wikipedia 15 kV AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15_kV_AC_railway_electrification)), and 25 Hz on Amtrak's southern Northeast Corridor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak%27s_25_Hz_traction_power_system)). OSM tags electrified lines with their frequency ([OSM Key:frequency](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/Key:frequency)), so a detected traction comb, using the 33.3/66.7 Hz spacing because phone high-passes remove the 16.7 Hz fundamental, puts the recorder within an estimated tens to hundreds of metres of a matching line. **GSM "bumblebee" buzz** at 216.7 Hz with dense harmonics ([US 7519347](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7519347)) means a 2G handset was transmitting nearby. That bounds the date to before the local switch-off (US T-Mobile 3 Aug 2026; France December 2026; Germany planned 30 Jun 2028) ([findcheapbroadband](https://findcheapbroadband.com/blog/2g-3g-switch-off-dates-worldwide/); [SMSEagle](https://www.smseagle.eu/europes-2g-and-3g-shutdown-calendar/)). No forensic precedent for this dating use was found. CRT line whine (15,625 Hz for 50 Hz-field video standards vs 15,734 Hz for 60 Hz) tags region and era, and gives a free ppm estimate of sample-rate error, but AAC upload low-passes usually erase it. Overhead-line corona hum (120 Hz, decaying about **3 dBA per doubling of distance** as a line source, mainly in wet weather) ([CPUC](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/environment/info/panoramaenv/tl695_tl6971/FMND/Pendleton_FMND_3.12_Noise.pdf)) is a proximity mask against OSM `power=line`. Lightning sferics would be the best clock of all, since **NLDN locates strokes to 300–500 m with 90–95% detection** ([Demetriades WMO 2010](https://old.wmo.int/extranet/pages/prog/www/IMOP/publications/IOM-104_TECO-2010/P2_3_Demetriades_USA.pdf)), and ten matched clicks would fix t0 to about 1 ms **[derived]**. But no case was found of a phone MEMS microphone picking up sferics, so this remains plausible but unproven. Direction from EM pickup is impractical with phone microphones, which have no induction coil.

### Logged acoustic sources share the clock with every aircraft

Every non-aircraft source reduces to an event term ℓ_e(x, τ, ε, θ_e) that shares the receiver position and clock with the aircraft events. That is the N-event joint likelihood the project already plans, extended through a source-agnostic catalogue loader. **Thunder** has the strongest data. With NLDN's **250 m median location error** ([Nag et al. 2014](https://www.vaisala.com/sites/default/files/documents/Nag%20et%20al-Recent%20Evolution%20of%20the%20U.S.%20National%20Lightning%20Detection%20Network-2014-ILDC-ILMC.pdf)), the per-strike timing σ is about 0.6 s. With GLD360 or Blitzortung (median 1.7 km in Germany, mean up to 5.6 km in Japan) it is 6–30 s **[derived]** ([VDE ICLP 2024](https://www.vde-verlag.de/proceedings-en/456416066.html); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8fc7d19f9cea4b2da32a19a6adeef740)). Even one strike collapses the aircraft clock/along-track degeneracy to a range circle, and thunderprint's channel-geometry model can be reused for onset timing. **Scheduled sounds** are cheap absolute clocks. The Netherlands tests **4,278 sirens at 12:00 on the first Monday of each month** for 1 min 26 s ([NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/node/57355)). Switzerland tests on the first Wednesday of February and Germany on the national Warntag ([alert.swiss](https://alert.swiss/en/precaution/testing-sirens.html)). German church clocks are mostly DCF77 radio-synced ([evangelisch.de](https://www.evangelisch.de/inhalte/142837/23-03-2017/sommerzeit-kommt-meist-funksignal-kirchtuerme)). Falcon 9 landing booms propagate stably to **at least 25 km** ([BYU](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/overview-of-byus-acoustical-measurements-of-the-falcon-9-sarah-1-launch-reentry-)). **Trains** run on a known 1-D curve, so a pass gives range to the track. US FRA rules require horns to sound **15–20 s before a public crossing until the lead unit occupies it** ([FRA](https://railroads.dot.gov/railroad-safety/divisions/crossing-safety-and-trespass-prevention/train-horn-rulequiet-zones)). Combined with the wheel CPA of the same train, that gives the receiver's along-track offset from a mapped crossing without needing any clock. GB TRUST/TD feeds give timestamps, but the only historical archive found is a 2018–2020 research dump ([Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/480416/1/Readme.txt)). One practitioner warns that a locomotive's Doppler-derived speed (125 km/h) disagreed with its timed speed (170 km/h) ([IOA, Rock](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/G%20ROCK%20RAILWAY%20NOISE%20-%20CALCULATION%20AND%20MEASUREMENT.pdf)), so axle-impulse timing is preferable to tonal Doppler for trains. **Ships** have free 1-minute AIS in the US ([MarineCadastre](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/national-ais-at-1-minute-intervals)) and COLREGS whistle bands by vessel length. Shoreline recordings get the mirror resolved for free, since one side of the lane is water. Road pass-bys give speed to about **7.9 km/h mean error** from one microphone ([arXiv 2204.04013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04013v1)), but they are numerous and indistinguishable, so they only add a road-distance mask. Wind-turbine amplitude modulation was audible **20% of the time at 2.4 km and 16% at 3.5 km** ([Flinders 2019](https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2019/06/19/wind-farm-noise-recorded-almost-9km-away/)). Against the ±10 m USWTDB positions, that gives a disc constraint and a negative-evidence term. Clock drift has to stay in the joint state: **[derived]** a siren onset 20 minutes before an aircraft CPA at 1000 ppm drift is a 1.2 s error. Because a false time anchor is worse than none, each anchor detector needs its own precision/recall measurement on the corpus before its term enters the likelihood.

### Natural cues narrow region and season, not c̄

Tree crickets are real thermometers. For *Oecanthus rileyi*, **T(°C) ≈ chirps per 11 s + 4** ([SINA](https://orthsoc.org/sina/588thermometer.htm)), and the species-group lines converge near 4 °C ([Walker & Collins 2010](https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-orthoptera-research/volume-19/issue-2/034.019.0227/New-World-Thermometer-Crickets--The-Oecanthus-rileyi-Species-Group/10.1665/034.019.0227.full)). They measure ground-level night air, though, which is exactly when inversions make path-mean T several K warmer. They constrain which reanalysis cell and hour applies, not c̄ directly. Cicadas thermoregulate and make poor thermometers ([EJE 2011](https://www.eje.cz/artkey/eje-201103-0005.php)). BirdNET's geomodel maps (lat, lon, week) to 12,012 class occurrence scores in a 7.1 MB ONNX file ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/sammlapp/BirdNET_GeoModel/blob/main/README.md)). Field precision is only **0.57–0.71 and recall 0.24–0.52 across continents** ([Funosas et al. 2026](https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/a-global-assessment-of-birdnet-performance-differences-among-cont/)), so detections must be tempered and absences heavily discounted. Dawn-chorus onset clusters **22–47 minutes before sunrise** ([J. Ornithol. 2020](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10336-020-01842-0)), a weak solar clock. Audio-derived wind speed reaches **1.17 m/s RMSE** on a fixed recorder ([Çoban et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16867v1)) but measures surface gusts, not the path-weighted along-track wind. It works best as a stability gate (calm suggests an inversion). The same study detects rain at **AUC 0.91–0.97**, a sharp filter against radar precipitation where weather is patchy. Its audio-to-temperature regressor has a 5.88 °C RMSE and should be ignored for c̄. Scene classification tops out near **58% for 10 classes** ([DCASE 2025](https://dcase.community/challenge2025/task-low-complexity-acoustic-scene-classification-with-device-information-results)). Spoken-language ID has **6.7% error across 107 languages** ([VoxLingua107 model](https://huggingface.co/TalTechNLP/voxlingua107-epaca-tdnn-ce)), decisive for Finnish and useless for English. Estimating humidity from how fast an aircraft's high harmonics fade has precedent only in 1970s calibrated certification work ([DTIC AD0754270](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD0754270.pdf)); with uncalibrated phones it yields coarse classes at best.

### Location priors stay weak, floored and ablatable

Geotagged media are **"highly imbalanced, with strong peaks in densely populated areas"** ([PlaNet](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1602.05314)). A constant urban prior is a meaningful baseline only because test sets are urban-skewed ([Bianco et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13862)). Doppler-rich clips, though, come from back gardens under approach paths, rural roads and airfield fences. The researchers recommend a mixture p(x) ∝ ε + w·LandScan(x)^γ + road/building terms, with γ ≈ 0.3–0.5, a uniform floor holding 10–20% of the mass over land, and a single tempering exponent τ fitted on the truth-cell log probability. It should be applied once per cell in stage 5 or 7 and never in pruning stages, and stratified in eval by the urbanicity of the true location. The prior is worth nearly nothing when one mode dominates, and decisive only for mirror and along-track ties. An aerodrome-proximity term would double-count what the likelihood already rewards. The hard physical constraints are cheaper and stronger: pin the observer to DEM height + 1.5 m (or +10 m for a building), apply a water mask with a finite penalty (around −5 nats rather than −∞), and use DEM line of sight in the silence stage. `sim` truth locations should be drawn 50/50 from uniform-on-land and population-weighted distributions, so that a prior cannot look good merely because the simulated truths happen to match it.

## Nine recordings cannot validate this; `sim` and stage-wise recall must

Corpus size, not access to data, is the binding constraint. **[derived, `ss.py`]** Exact Clopper–Pearson intervals give n = 9 a ±0.3 band on a hit rate. A ±0.10 half-width at 0.8 needs 60–70 independent recordings, and telling 0.80 from 0.90 needs about 200. Exact McNemar has **37% power at n = 40** for a 15-point improvement. A coverage test at n = 50 detects a true 80% coverage of a nominal 90% region only 56% of the time. No airport noise-monitoring programme publishes bulk raw audio linked to tracks. The one new usable source is a **CC BY 4.0 Sussex sample of 98 per-aircraft MP3s recorded near Gatwick, with a position CSV** ([Zenodo 21072869](https://zenodo.org/records/21072869)). It does not state the device, time sync or recorder coordinates, so the curator has to be asked. The statistical recipe follows. Use continuous proper scores as primary metrics: the log score at the true cell, and the energy score, which is strictly proper in ℝ² ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). Compare variants with paired bootstrap or exact sign-flip tests per recording, clustered by session ([Dror et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.01448)). Report tiered success at 100 m, 300 m, 1 km and 3 km, as Sattler et al. did for visual localisation, never scoring a case finer than its own truth uncertainty ([arXiv 1707.09092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.09092)). Pre-register the hypothesis, primary metric and minimum effect in each `experiments/*.toml`. Keep a frozen, hash-listed test split that is used only when a variant is promoted to default. The cheapest guardrail is **stage-wise recall**: did the truth survive event detection, association, the CPA-table bounds, the 200 m and 100 m stages, and what was its final rank? Because DSP bounds only prune, a regression shows up there first, as a binary signal.

`sim` has to carry the fine-grained work, and it must be made able to fail. Simulation-based calibration ([Talts et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788)) passes whenever `sim` and the inference model share the same misspecification, and several SBI methods are known to be overconfident ([Hermans et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581v1)). Thunderprint hit exactly this: its twin "cannot reward refraction physics by construction" ([TP thunder_physics_lit.md](../../reference/thunderprint/research/thunder_physics_lit.md)). doppler_print's `sim` has one c and one wind vector, so it cannot test the c̄/w̄ prescription. The earlier report's [SIM] tables were produced by scripts that shared the fitted model's straight-ray structure. The auralisation literature names the realism ingredients `sim` lacks. Turbulence-induced **coherence loss on the ground reflection** is the first: expert listeners picked the coherence-loss version as closer to recordings 87.5% of the time ([Pieren & Lincke, JASA 2022](https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/151/4/2453/2838204/Auralization-of-aircraft-flyovers-with-turbulence)), whereas `sim` uses a perfectly coherent mirror that makes Lloyd's-mirror fringes too regular. Others are amplitude and phase fluctuation on every path, source AM/FM measured from real engines ([Rizzi & Sahai, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20200002351)), and curved rays. These can come from the open ITA atmospheric ray tracer, run offline into a lookup table ([DAGA 2021](https://pub.dega-akustik.de/DAGA_2021/data/articles/000197.pdf)), or from `sim`'s own layered Snell shooting. Either way, `physics::emission` and `physics::doppler_factors` are never reused. Sim-to-real fidelity should be judged by posterior predictive checks on evidence statistics (ridge SNR against slant, tracked fraction, IF residual, spurious ridges per minute, fringe depth) rather than by listening tests ([Gelman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01808)).

## Thunderprint transfers as method more than physics

Thunderprint's one large, measured physical win was the **effective CG onset height**. Fitting it as a nuisance cut real-clip error from **2.64 to 1.33 km**, but on a single clip, not blind, and the twin sim could not reproduce it ([TP empirical_thunder_response.md](../../reference/thunderprint/research/empirical_thunder_response.md)). Refraction, terrain shielding and clock drift were tested on that clip and ruled out. Lapse overrides gave 2.55–2.89 km against 2.64 km, so the refraction shadow ships off by default, and the README's claim that refraction drove the model overstates it. Wind and temperature were marginalised over a 27-atmosphere grid, but no A/B result was recorded. The processing findings transfer best. A **count-based reliability gate** separated correct picks (≥76 strikes heard) from wrong ones (≤21) **"while fit and posterior did not separate them"**. **Huber rescoring "lifted false candidates as much as the truth (no gain)"**. Likelihood tempering (n_eff factor 0.5) was calibrated against CR90 coverage ([TP shape.rs](../../reference/thunderprint/src/search/shape.rs); [TP config.rs](../../reference/thunderprint/src/config.rs)).

| Thunderprint finding | Transfers to doppler_print? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Onset height (extended source) | Physics no, **method yes** | Aircraft are compact sources with ADS-B altitude; the transferable part is a truth-referenced residual study on real passes (near-minus-far differential, slant/elevation binning) |
| Refraction as timing bias | **No** | Chord-with-profile ≤ 8 m GA, ≤ 25 m jets **[derived]**; temperature only, shear unverified |
| Refraction as audibility | **Yes** | Shadow edge √(2RH) at 3–23 km feeds P_d; ship behind an override, since TP's amplitude version showed no gain |
| Wind dominance (Anderson thesis) | Yes, already ranked | Consistent with the split above; TP adds no number |
| Count-based reliability gate | **Yes** | Threshold distinct matched passes on a deliberately unlocatable `sim` tier |
| n_eff / double counting | **Yes** | Harmonics and split segments of one pass are not independent events |
| Huber at search level | **Caution** | TP saw no gain; the earlier report's rank-8 Huber is at the event fit, a different stage, so A/B it |
| Stereo DOA | Not as default | A few nats in TP; here, keep only the mirror bit |
| Clock day-scan + video≠audio clock | Yes | TP's 4-minute "no drift" result does not cover hour-long files; keep ε |
| Loudness refit, hot-loop ray tracing | No | No separating power / chord model suffices for temperature |

The deepest lesson is TP's own trap. Its only big win came from regressing **real** audio against the catalogue, which the sim could not reveal. doppler_print's closest candidates for a similar surprise are source and data systematics: slant-correlated ADS-B latency, baro/geometric datum slips, prop f0 drift with aspect, and multipath ridges mistaken for direct ones. An `empirical_doppler_response.md` study on the corpus clips that have truth is cheap and is where any such surprise would show up.

Several tensions between the two rounds remain open and should be settled by the experiments below, not by argument. The earlier report paired c̄ and w̄ as one top item; this round splits them, because temperature is cheap to ±2 K while nocturnal wind is not. The ray-trace check moves refraction out of the bias budget and into the audibility budget, but only for temperature profiles: shear, the earlier report's second-largest term, is still unverified against curved rays. The earlier report proposed Huber at the event fit, and thunderprint measured no gain from Huber at search level. Those are different stages, but the burden of proof now rests on Huber. Thunderprint also found that fit quality did not separate right from wrong answers while source count did, which challenges any reliability metric built on residuals alone. Finally, the earlier report's "SIM within ×2 of the tables" acceptance criterion is weaker than it looked: those tables and the current `sim` share the straight-ray, uniform-medium assumption, so agreement would not prove the c̄/w̄ prescription correct.

The `sim` extensions are as follows. Each must be built from `sim`'s own propagation code, independent of `physics`. First, an optional `[atmosphere] c_profile` / `wind_profile` with layered Snell shooting and missing arrivals in shadow, exercised by `stratified_isa_jet.toml`, `inversion_ga300.toml` (expect shadow beyond ≈3.2 km), `shear_along_jet.toml` and `shear_cross_ga.toml`. Wind needs a proper moving-medium ray model; the effective-sound-speed shortcut failed. Second, ground-reflection coherence C_coh ∈ {1, 0.5, 0} at f ≥ 250 Hz with a Q sweep. Third, turbulent amplitude and phase jitter as a sweep, not a constant. Fourth, a source AM/FM library fitted from the Livermore cases, plus β drift. Fifth, injected untracked aircraft and non-aircraft tonal clutter, to measure λ_u, λ_c and false-silence rates. Sixth, injected hum with ENF wander, a gated 216.7 Hz comb and a 15.625 kHz line, put through AAC/Opus round-trips. Seventh, independently synthesised anchors (siren test, train with horn, thunder). Eighth, a two-channel stereo render for the mirror test. Ninth, truth locations sampled 50/50 uniform/population. And last, a deliberately unlocatable tier for thresholding the reliability gate. The derivation scripts behind this round's [derived] numbers are in [`scripts/`](<../../research_notes/Doppler localization deeper factors/scripts/>). ## Conclusion

The question has moved from "how accurate is the physics" to "which independent bit of information removes which ambiguity". The first round showed that atmospheric bias hides in single-event residuals. This round shows that, of the atmospheric terms, only wind stays expensive. Refraction matters through where aircraft can be heard rather than through the curve fit, and the remaining degeneracies fall only to evidence of a particular kind: one bit of handedness for the mirror, and an absolute clock or scene position for along-track. That makes doppler_print's natural extension a fusion engine more than a better Doppler fitter: one shared clock and position, many catalogues (ADS-B, MLAT, FLARM, lightning, sirens, rail, AIS), and explicit "unknown" and "deaf" hypotheses so that missing data cannot masquerade as evidence.

The second implication is methodological, and thunderprint already paid for it. A twin simulator that shares the model's assumptions certifies the model's blind spots, and the only large surprise thunderprint found came from real audio regressed against truth. doppler_print should therefore make `sim` able to fail (stratified wind, shadows, coherence loss, untracked traffic) before adopting any of the atmospheric or silence defaults. In parallel, it should mine its few truth-labelled real clips for systematics that no derivation predicts. With nine real cases, every promotion decision rests on `sim` plus paired, pre-registered comparisons, so building the evaluation harness comes first in the roadmap.
