# Aircraft Doppler geolocation inverts thirty-year-old physics

The physics behind colsto's method is old and well tested. **Fitting a single microphone's propeller Doppler ridge for speed, closest-approach (CPA) range, CPA time and rest frequency dates to Ferguson's DSTO work in the 1990s.** Seismologists now routinely match aircraft Doppler glides on nodal arrays to Flightradar24 and GPS tracks. What nobody had published before colsto is the inversion: treating ADS-B aircraft as known sources and solving for an **unknown recorder** anywhere in a country. The only precedents for that are in radio (ADS-B multilateration receivers that calibrate their clocks and positions from aircraft broadcasts) and in satellite Doppler positioning. Within days of the video, at least four open-source reconstructions appeared. None has shown a blind localisation on real audio. Two independent synthetic studies agree that one flyover without a tight time window cannot fix a position: it leaves an along-track and a left/right mirror ambiguity. Audio forensics as a field answers *when* well (ENF mains hum to about 1 s, broadcast fingerprints) and *where* only coarsely (power-grid region, or city level at roughly 50% accuracy). That makes those techniques complementary time priors for doppler_print, not competitors. The best ideas to borrow come from other domains. Satellite Doppler (Transit, COSPAS-SARSAT) handles the per-pass frequency offset and the mirror ambiguity. Continuous gravitational-wave searches provide mismatch-bounded coarse-to-fine grids and analytically maximised nuisance parameters. ADS-B multilateration networks provide receiver clock calibration from known aircraft. Statistical framing, validation corpora and ethics are covered in [prior_art_validation.md](../research/prior_art_validation.md) and are not repeated here.

## Single-microphone Doppler fitting is settled science; only the inversion is new

The constant-velocity fit in doppler_print's event stage has a direct ancestor. Ferguson and Quinn analysed a propeller aircraft's narrowband blade-rate line at a ground sensor and compared the short-time Fourier transform with the Wigner-Ville distribution for extracting the ridge ([Macquarie](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/application-of-the-short-time-fourier-transform-and-the-wigner-vi/)). Ferguson's 1995 ASA abstract states the principle outright: with **one sensor**, the Doppler effect "is exploited to localize aircraft having narrow-band lines in their source spectra". It also notes that fast aircraft need short integration times ([ASA 4pSP10](https://auditory.org/asamtgs/asa95stl/4pSP/4pSP10.html)). His companion work on jets used cross-correlation of two microphones in broad bands, because jets lack a clean tonal line ([ASA 1pEA17](https://auditory.org/asamtgs/asa95wsh/1pEA/1pEA17.html)). That is independent support for doppler_print's props-only filter.

Modern versions refine the estimator but keep the same model. Yu, Xiao and Sun recover f0, speed, altitude and closest horizontal distance from a single hydrophone. They use the Doppler curve *and its first and second derivatives*, and add an instantaneous-frequency tracker for non-Gaussian noise. On a turboprop recording they get f0 ≈ 68.5 Hz and v ≈ 101 m/s, but they **report no ground-truth error** ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.996493/full)). The few accuracy benchmarks that exist are sobering. A Moscow University study found **under 10% speed error** from single-point Doppler. Position, however, needed **three measurement points** ([Springer 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3103/S0027134920050082)). A DRDO single-microphone UAV fit produced altitude and speed estimates without any ground-truth comparison ([DSJ](https://publications.drdo.gov.in/ojs/index.php/dsj/article/download/2238/1198/7489)). A 13-microphone CAS array added bearing from phase to the same CPA triple ([CAS, snippet only](https://english.cas.cn/newsroom/archive/research_archive/rp2016/201608/t20160823_166673.shtml)). TU Delft benchmarked 64-microphone beamforming of airport flyovers against **ADS-B Out positions** ([TU Delft](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/a-comparison-of-source-localization-methods-with-varying-sizes-of/)). That is ADS-B as acoustic ground truth, but still in the forward direction.

Seismology is the closest academic neighbour, and it runs the forward problem at scale. Meng and Ben-Zion catalogued **1,816 air-traffic events** on the Anza nodal array and published MATLAB code for modelling their Doppler ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/kpftptxdj5)). Eibl et al. recovered helicopter blade count, rotor rpm, speed, heading, altitude and position from seismometers, and validated the results against the helicopter's GPS track ([UCD](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/entities/publication/a6c4c008-8937-4b93-b045-6623d442d8cc)). The University of Alaska Fairbanks group used 303 nodes along the Parks Highway. They matched each Doppler curve to Flightradar24, removed the Doppler shift to expose the engine's harmonic comb, and classified aircraft as piston, turboprop or jet across 16 types ([UAF](https://www.uaf.edu/news/uaf-study-seismic-data-can-identify-aircraft-by-type.php)). This is the nearest prior art to colsto's matching step. However, the press release gives no accuracy figures, and the sensor position is always known. Wang et al. (2026) extend the single-seismometer fit with an **acceleration term** for aircraft taking off and landing ([Earthquake Research Advances](https://www.synapsesocial.com/papers/69d8930e6c1944d70ce04226)). That bears directly on the manoeuvring segment that broke colsto's event 1. Supersonic work localises trajectories from boom arrival times on seismic networks ([USGS OFR 91-48](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1991/0048/report.pdf)) and cross-bears booms from two infrasound arrays ([EGU21-1366](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-1366.html)). One patent predicts boom footprints directly from ADS-B ground speed and an assumed wind profile ([US11715381](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11715381)).

Defence and patent work points the same way: sensors locate sources. DJI's expired patent combines MUSIC direction finding, the Doppler sign (approaching or receding) and intensity-versus-distance for a known source type ([US10473752B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10473752B2/en)). That last element is a cousin of doppler_print's audibility (silence) model. ARL's Persistent Harmonic Acoustic Detection tracks rotorcraft and UAVs by their harmonic combs on sparse networks ([DTIC AD1111070](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1111070.pdf)). The receiver-from-known-aircraft inversion appears only in radio. Examples include phase calibration of meteor-radar interferometers from ADS-B aircraft ([URSI 2024](https://www.usnc-ursi-archive.org/nrsm/2024/papers/1110.pdf)), an angle-of-arrival patent in which a receiver navigates from ADS-B-reported positions ([US10365374](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10365374)), and joint receiver calibration and target localisation for multilateration ([US9664770](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9664770)). Within the venues searched, **no paper locates an acoustic or seismic receiver from ADS-B-tracked aircraft Doppler**. One consequence goes unremarked in the defence literature: any unattended ground sensor near flight paths leaks its own position through what it records.

## Audio forensics answers "when" precisely and "where" only at grid scale

Electric network frequency analysis is the mature, court-tested chronolocation tool. Its matching structure is the same as doppler_print's: extract a one-dimensional trace from the spectrogram, slide it against a GPS-timed catalogue, and take the correlation peak. Mains frequency wanders identically across a synchronous grid ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_network_frequency_analysis)). Grigoras's "ENF Criterion" gives recording time and detects edits ([IJSLL](https://journal.equinoxpub.com/IJSLL/article/view/9977)). Matching against references such as FNET reaches **about 1 s** timing under good conditions. Clip length matters most, and SNR decides whether matching works at all ([arXiv 2011.00176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00176)). The ENF literature's central warning applies directly to doppler_print: **short clips produce false peaks** because the reference signal is self-similar ([AAFS 2009](https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2009-B20.pdf)). Raw match scores therefore mean little without a null distribution. As a location tool, ENF is coarse. Grid classification without a concurrent reference reaches about **93% on power signals and 80% on audio** ([arXiv 1912.09428](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.09428)). Intra-grid location with anchor sites reaches about 90% only under favourable conditions ([ICASSP 2013](https://www.mast.umd.edu/files/paper_ENF/conf_paper6_ICASSP13.pdf)). The robust-extraction work behind the ENF-WHU benchmark finds that **combining several harmonics, weighted by local SNR, beats any single harmonic** ([arXiv 2011.03414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.03414)). That carries over directly to propeller blade-pass harmonics. ENF also has a counter-forensic weakness: it can be removed and replaced ([USC thesis](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/doctoral/Multimedia-Forensics-and-Security-Using-Electric/991191845902621)).

The other forensic families do one of two jobs. Some verify a location already proposed. Others supply weak regional priors. Room fingerprints ("Roomprints" identified the room in **96%** of trials ([Imperial](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/06a2cafd-39b1-440f-add2-214ca96b86b2))) and microphone fingerprints both need a reference recording from the candidate room or device, so they fit at the end of a pipeline, like colsto's Street View check. The microphone work does teach one lesson: device frequency response and noise distort harmonic amplitudes ([arXiv 2206.11640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.11640)). Scoring should therefore be invariant to per-band gain. Learned soundscape geolocation is coarse. City classification sits around **50–56%** ([QMUL](https://soundscape.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/2019/05/02/pre-print-of-new-work-released-sound-scene-city-classification-audio-geotagging/index.html)). Audio-visual video geolocation places only about **1% of clips within 1 km** ([La Salle](https://blogs.salleurl.edu/en/node/11461)). The 2026 AGL1K benchmark finds that audio-language models lean mostly on speech and language cues ([arXiv 2601.03227](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03227v1)). Physics-based catalogue matching is orthogonal to all of these, which strengthens the case for doppler_print. Bioacoustic species-range priors are a useful soft input for both region and season ([arXiv 2505.18726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18726v1)). Background broadcast audio matched against monitoring archives, which store hash, channel and timestamp ([US9451048](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9451048)), gives time plus a coverage area. Detection of background music is weak, though: no algorithm exceeded **F1 = 47%** on the BAF benchmark ([ISMIR 2022](https://ismir2022program.ismir.net/poster_228.html)).

Catalogue-matched *events* are the closest structural relatives of doppler_print. ShotSpotter places **96% of shots within 15 m when six or more sensors participate**, and multipath is the dominant error ([arXiv 2108.07377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.07377)). For a single microphone, several aircraft events from different directions play the role of extra sensors. Lightning networks such as NLDN and Blitzortung supply thunderprint's catalogue ([Blitzortung](https://www.blitzortung.org/cover_your_area.php)). For scheduled or tracked sounds (train horns against timetables, bells, siren tests, **ship horns against AIS**), the search found **no peer-reviewed forensic literature**. Ship noise plus AIS is the most direct untapped twin: moving tonal sources that broadcast their own positions to a public log.

| Technique | Answers | Best reported precision | Role for doppler_print |
|---|---|---|---|
| ENF mains hum | When; which grid | ~1 s; 80% grid ID from audio | Time prior and grid mask |
| Broadcast fingerprint | When; coverage area | ~1 s, but F1 < 47% in background | Time prior |
| Room / mic fingerprint | Same place or device? | 96% room ID (closed set) | Final verification |
| Learned soundscape | Which city/region | ~50–56% city accuracy | Soft spatial prior |
| Gunshot multilateration | Source location | 15 m with ≥6 sensors | Geometry-vs-accuracy lesson |
| Aircraft Doppler + ADS-B | Where and when | ~100 m claimed (colsto, unvalidated) | The method itself |

## OSINT investigators locate the shooter, never the recorder

Published newsroom audio work almost always takes the camera position as known and solves for the source. In the Shireen Abu Akleh killing, Forensic Architecture and Al-Haq traced all 13 shots to one position ([FA/Al-Haq](https://content.forensic-architecture.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2022.11.02_Final-Audio-and-Acoustic-Analysis-SAA-Report.pdf)). Robert Maher's crack-bang analysis for CNN gave about 200 m and was later published with uncertainty ranges ([Maher AES](https://ougfc.montana.edu/rmaher/publications/maher_aes_1022_22.pdf)). Bellingcat's 2025 Gaza aid-worker investigation combined crack-bang ranging (about 30–45 m), SunCalc chronolocation and SkySat imagery ([Bellingcat](https://bellingcat.com/news/2025/04/09/gaza-palestine-israel-killed-death-aid-workers-international-red-cross-crescent-audio-analysis)). The nearest newsroom analogue to a Doppler fit is Earshot's drone analysis, which "tracks changes in pitch and volume to reconstruct a drone's movements". In the Issam Abdallah case it showed a drone circling for 23 minutes. Earshot reports its findings as probabilities ([Earshot](https://earshotngo.substack.com/p/how-sounds-help-solve-crimes)). A search snippet suggests that NTSB "Sound Spectrum" docket reports have checked propeller speed from witness phone videos against ADS-B. The PDF text could not be extracted, so this is **unconfirmed** ([NTSB ERA23FA138](https://data.ntsb.gov/Docket/Document/docBLOB?ID=17512582&FileExtension=pdf&FileName=ERA23FA138-Spectrum+Report-FINAL-Rel.pdf)). If confirmed, it would be the closest institutional precedent.

ADS-B is a staple of OSINT, but it has always been used visually or for pattern-of-life tracking. Bellingcat's toolkit shows matching a FlightAware track to a contrail in a photo ([Bellingcat Toolkit](https://bellingcat.gitbook.io/toolkit/more/all-tools/flightaware)). Its Turnstone tool searches adsb.lol history for aircraft that appear in two regions within a maximum time gap ([Bellingcat Turnstone](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2026/03/05/turnstone-flight-tracking-tool/)), which is structurally the same as colsto's locked inter-event offset. BuzzFeed's "Spies in the Skies" found that about two dozen FBI and more than 130 DHS aircraft **never appeared on Flightradar24** ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/watchdog/how-buzzfeed-news-revealed-hidden-spy-planes-in-us-airspace.php)). That is direct evidence that a hard silence filter can veto the true location. No published case matched an *audible* aircraft to ADS-B before colsto.

colsto's channel previously published two thunder-geolocation videos ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6a7Z0I3LAnE)). The aircraft video explicitly released no code ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UytYrhYLOKI)). The reaction came fast and was positive. Simon Willison called it a "genius" piece of OSINT ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/a-sound-that-leaks-your-exact-location/)). Lobsters commenters said they had expected the video to be about ENF ([Lobsters](https://lobste.rs/s/s1btqo/this_background_sound_leaks_your_exact)). The most rigorous independent check is cbonitz's agent-built verification, which returned **"Not a hoax"**. With **two flyovers and an unknown time**, the correct flight pair ranked first in 6 of 6 synthetic worlds, with **85–830 m** error under realistic wind, temperature and ADS-B noise. A **single flight in a one-hour window failed in 6 of 6** ([cbonitz](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments)). cbonitz also found that ignoring emission (retarded) time "worsens the fit but doesn't make the search fail entirely". That contradicts colsto's framing. It also conflicts with the expectation in prior_art_validation.md that the emission-time ablation would fail outright, so doppler_print should measure this effect rather than assert it.

## Four reconstructions exist, none validated blind on real audio

The open-source field is young, Python-only for the aircraft method, and still unvalidated on real audio. DOPPLERGEIST is the most complete. It covers track discovery, flight ranking, terrain-aware refinement and tiled national search. It reports 21.9 m and 40.9 m on synthetic cases and 43.9 m on a real two-event clip, but its own README states **"no accepted blind real localization"** ([DOPPLERGEIST](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/DOPPLERGEIST); [benchmarks](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/DOPPLERGEIST/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md)). mojomast's Hermes plugin is the most compact readable chain: a Viterbi ridge, a flyover fit, and emission-time scoring. Without a time constraint it lands about **1.4 km along-track** and shows a mirror cluster across the track ([Hermes plugin](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation)). thunderprint is the only thunder implementation and the only one with a Rust core. It ships as a **wheel that bundles a CLI binary, not a PyO3 module** ([thunderprint](https://github.com/Hexay/thunderprint)), so doppler_print's PyO3 plan is a deliberate departure.

| Project | Language / licence | Scope | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [DOPPLERGEIST](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/DOPPLERGEIST) | Python / MIT | Full pipeline, tiled search, web app | 21.9–43.9 m synthetic and non-held-out cases; no blind real fix |
| [mojomast Hermes plugin](https://github.com/mojomast/hermes-aircraft-doppler-geolocation) | Python / MIT | Ridge → fit → score vs ADS-B CSV | Synthetic only; 1 m slant with known time |
| [cbonitz/doppler-experiments](https://github.com/cbonitz/doppler-experiments) | Python / none | Verification experiments | 6/6 two-event, 0/6 single-event (synthetic) |
| [SarpHarbali/aircraft-audio-geolocation](https://github.com/SarpHarbali/aircraft-audio-geolocation) | Python / none | Frames, synthetic flyovers, ADS-B schema | Phase 1 only |
| [thunderprint](https://github.com/Hexay/thunderprint) | Rust / MIT | Thunder vs lightning catalogue | 0.4–1.0 km synthetic; 1.3 km real, not blind |

Permissively licensed building blocks exist for almost every layer. On the Rust side there are rustfft/realfft, rstar/kiddo, georust gdal and geozero for GeoPackage output, and arrow-rs. On the Python side, xoolive/traffic is MIT and adsb.lol's ODbL globe_history archives are available ([adsb.lol globe_history_2026](https://github.com/adsblol/globe_history_2026); [traffic](https://github.com/xoolive/traffic)). There is **no notable Rust ridge-tracking or synchrosqueezing crate**, so the Viterbi ridge tracker has to be written in-house. ssqueezepy and librosa's pyin can serve as test oracles ([ssqueezepy](https://github.com/OverLordGoldDragon/ssqueezepy)). The licence traps are pyModeS and readsb (GPL), pyopensky (its licence file and PyPI metadata conflict), and JoulesSpace's otherwise attractive no_std blade-pass DSP crates (AGPL) ([JoulesSpace](https://github.com/JoulesSpace/acoustic-drone-detection)). For real ADS-B-labelled audio, AeroSonicDB offers 625 low-altitude recordings near Adelaide airport ([arXiv 2311.06368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06368)). Planefence plus NoiseCapt is the working template for a self-built microphone + receiver corpus ([docker-planefence](https://github.com/sdr-enthusiasts/docker-planefence)). The dense seismic datasets (Anza, Parks Highway) are an overlooked benchmark: **hide the station coordinates and see whether ADS-B matching recovers them**. No open repository combines ENF with any spatial localisation finer than grid region.

## Satellites, sonar and gravitational-wave searches already solved doppler_print's open problems

Satellite Doppler positioning has the same geometry as a static observer hearing one flyover. Transit solved the unknown oscillator offset jointly with position, iteratively from integrated Doppler, and reached about 20 m for a stationary receiver ([UNB TR29](https://gge.ext.unb.ca/Pubs/TR29.pdf)). A NIMBUS-6 study estimated **one offset per pass** ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19790007861/downloads/19790007861.pdf)). That is exactly doppler_print's per-event f0. COSPAS-SARSAT's LEOSAR processing always yields a **real/image pair** mirrored about the ground track and assigns each a probability. It declares the position resolved only when a second pass or an encoded GNSS fix agrees within a tolerance (documents give 20 km and 50 km) ([NOAA 2009](https://www.sarsat.noaa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2009-USA-Alert-Message-Formats.pdf)). Argos chooses between the two solutions using frequency-fit residuals ([JMA](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/mscweb/technotes/msctechrep20-1.pdf)). The single-flyover failures that cbonitz and mojomast reported are the satellite mirror problem, rediscovered. Psiaki's LEO Doppler solution adds clock-offset *rate* as an unknown ([Inside GNSS](https://insidegnss.com/using-leo-signals-of-opportunity-for-pnt/)). Time-diverse stacking lets fewer satellites suffice, but constant-velocity assumptions break over long spans ([DOT ROSAP](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/80647)). In doppler_print terms, that becomes an f0 drift term for RPM changes and a joint multi-event likelihood.

Emitter geolocation adds the scoring machinery. Direct Position Determination scores each position hypothesis straight from raw data, which is what "overlay the curve, sum the brightness" already does. Its key trick is to **maximise analytically over nuisance parameters** (a maximum-eigenvalue per grid point) instead of sweeping them ([MDPI Sensors](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/18/12/4139)). Grid-CAF geolocation precomputes replicas per grid point and draws error ellipses from the CRLB (HᵀC⁻¹H)⁻¹ ([US12028719](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12028719)). Derivative (Doppler-rate) observables tighten the CRLB cheaply ([arXiv 2604.14413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14413)). ADS-B multilateration networks are the radio twin of doppler_print. They calibrate receiver clocks from aircraft with known positions ([FlyItalyADSB](https://flyitalyadsb.com/en/community/mlat/)). The OpenSky localisation competition winner said that clock-drift modelling (linear plus random walk) and **data cleaning** mattered as much as the solver ([ZHAW](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/handle/11475/21210)).

Passive sonar adds lessons on robustness. Target motion analysis with an unknown rest frequency searches a bank of hypothesised frequencies ([US5432753](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5432753)). It also needs **multi-hypothesis initialisation**, because a single poor start fails to converge ([ISIF](https://isif.org/media/26)). Road constraints make Doppler-only ground-target tracking solvable ([IEEE Access](https://doaj.org/article/0b8c85fba4d248349755a6effcf2c325)), the analogue of a land/building prior on the observer. Ship-noise inversion shows that a **joint constant-velocity track model beats sequential independent inversions** ([Acta Physica Sinica](https://wulixb.iphy.ac.cn/en/article/doi/10.7498/aps.65.164304)). Matched-field processing warns that sharp high-resolution processors are "extremely susceptible to mismatch". Bartlett (summed-power) scoring tolerates mismatch better, and the ambiguity surfaces have structured sidelobes ([ASA 1994](https://auditory.org/asamtgs/asa94mit/4pUW/4pUW12.html)).

The closest methodological twin is the continuous gravitational-wave search. It hunts a weak tone whose received frequency is Doppler-modulated by known motion, across an enormous parameter grid. CW pipelines place templates using a **parameter-space metric** so that the worst-case mismatch is bounded ([arXiv 1804.03392](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.03392)). They run semi-coherent coarse stages before coherent fine stages ([arXiv 1510.03417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03417)) and follow up survivors with MCMC ([arXiv 1802.05450](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.05450)). This addresses a known flaw in colsto's pipeline: a fixed 200 m coarse grid can leave the truth 141 m from the nearest node, where it scores badly. Because f(t) = f0·g(t; x), f0 is a pure scale factor. On a log-frequency spectrogram it becomes a shift, so it can be concentrated out with a single FFT cross-correlation, the same move as the F-statistic.

## What is genuinely new and what is borrowed

Little in colsto's pipeline is new apart from the direction of inference and the national-scale search. The individual components all have decades of precedent. The contested modelling detail, retarded time, matters less than claimed. The real gaps lie in confidence, ambiguity handling and catalogue generality.

| Element | Status | Nearest precedent |
|---|---|---|
| Single-mic Doppler fit (f0, v, CPA range/time) | Established since the 1990s | Ferguson (DSTO); Yu et al. 2022 |
| Matching Doppler curves to commercial flight tracks | Established (sensor known) | Seppi et al. / UAF 2025; Eibl et al. 2017 |
| Receiver position/clock from known aircraft | Established in RF only | ADS-B MLAT calibration; US10365374 |
| Per-pass offset nuisance; mirror-pair ambiguity | Established in satellite Doppler | Transit, NIMBUS, COSPAS-SARSAT |
| Catalogue-correlation timestamping of audio | Established | ENF, broadcast monitoring |
| **Acoustic receiver geolocation by ADS-B Doppler search over a country** | **Novel (colsto, 2026)** | None peer-reviewed |
| Retarded-time correction | Physically correct, but not decisive | cbonitz: fit degrades, search survives |
| Silence (negative-evidence) filter | No direct precedent found | Loosely: SARSAT "no second detection", road priors |
| **Calibrated confidence, ambiguity modes, blind real validation** | **Open: nobody ships them** | See prior_art_validation.md §4 |
| Same engine on ships + AIS, trains + timetables | Open, no literature found | thunderprint (lightning) |

## Prioritised design takeaways for doppler_print

The first tier fixes correctness and confidence, where all existing reconstructions are weakest. The second improves accuracy and speed. The third widens scope. Items already specified in prior_art_validation.md (time-shift nulls, decoys, the validation ladder, ethics) are referenced, not restated.

| # | Takeaway | Borrowed from | Concrete change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Report ambiguity modes, not an argmax | COSPAS-SARSAT, Argos, Hermes/cbonitz | For each event, emit the mirror and along-track modes with probabilities. Mark "resolved" only when an independent event's mode lands within a CRLB-scaled tolerance. Break mirror ties with fit residuals, not just brightness. Complements prior_art_validation.md design item 4. |
| 2 | Concentrate f0 (and small time offsets) out of the search | F-statistic, DPD eigenvalue trick | Resample to log-frequency and score f0 as a 1-D FFT correlation per (cell, flight). Keep one f0 per event, plus an optional linear drift term (Psiaki's clock-rate analogue). |
| 3 | Replace the fixed 200→100 m grid with a mismatch-bounded anisotropic lattice | CW template metrics, coarse/fine hierarchies | Derive g_ij from the score Hessian per flight. Use spacing that is tight across-track and loose along-track, sized for a bounded score loss. Finish with Nelder–Mead or MCMC in continuous (lat, lon, f0, offset). Keep the top-K modes per stage (TMA multi-hypothesis). |
| 4 | Score all events in one joint likelihood | Time-diverse LEO Doppler, ship-track inversion, MLAT | Use N-event joint scoring with one shared clock offset when a timestamp exists, plus a linear audio-clock drift term, enabled only when residuals call for it. |
| 5 | Make the spectrogram score mismatch-tolerant and gain-invariant | MFP Bartlett, ENF-WHU, mic fingerprinting, CFAR | Incoherent, SNR-weighted sum across harmonics k·f0. Per-band normalisation. Subtract an off-curve local background. Huber or trimmed residuals for manoeuvring segments. |
| 6 | Emit CRLB ellipses and a flight-informativeness map | Grid-CAF patent, DOP analysis | Use the Jacobian of the retarded-time model with respect to observer position to drive QGIS error ellipses, adaptive grid spacing, and pre-ranking of which flights are worth scoring. |
| 7 | Clean and spline ADS-B before the retarded-time solve | OpenSky localisation competition | Reject outliers, fix position jumps, smooth velocity. Noise in v_r feeds directly into the predicted curve. |
| 8 | Model manoeuvres, not just constant velocity | Wang et al. 2026, Yu et al. 2022 | Use the real trajectory for prediction (already planned). In the event-fit pre-filter, add acceleration and curve-asymmetry terms so turning or climbing aircraft are not rejected. |
| 9 | Measure, don't assert, the retarded-time effect | cbonitz | Make the emission-time ablation a reported benchmark row (fit RMS and rank of truth), not an expected failure. |
| 10 | Benchmark on hidden-coordinate seismic arrays and AeroSonicDB | Meng & Ben-Zion, UAF, AeroSonicDB | Hide station or microphone coordinates and recover them. Use the Hermes plugin and ssqueezepy as test oracles for the in-house Rust Viterbi ridge. |
| 11 | Accept external time priors | ENF, broadcast monitoring | An optional ENF pre-pass (60/120/180 Hz) gives a time window and grid mask. No free historical North American grid-frequency archive was verified, so ship it as a pluggable prior, not a core dependency. |
| 12 | Abstract the catalogue loader | thunderprint, ship-noise + AIS literature | Keep the Doppler engine source-agnostic so AIS ships and timetabled trains become loaders. This is the clearest unclaimed niche. |

## Conclusion

The novelty in colsto's work is the question it asks. Seismologists, sonar engineers and satellite navigators had all built the forward machinery and the receiver-calibration machinery separately. Nobody had pointed an ADS-B catalogue at an unknown microphone. Because the physics is borrowed, the failure modes are borrowed too. The single-flyover along-track and mirror ambiguity is COSPAS-SARSAT's real/image pair. The coarse-grid miss is a CW template-bank mismatch problem. The "20.48 vs 15" margin is an ENF short-clip false peak waiting to happen. Each field has a known fix, and none of the four aircraft reconstructions applies them.

doppler_print's advantage therefore lies in importing those fixes rather than in faster Rust hot loops. Concretely, that means a per-event f0 nuisance concentrated out of the likelihood, a metric-shaped lattice with bounded mismatch, explicit ambiguity modes, a joint multi-event likelihood, and CRLB ellipses in the QGIS output. Once those are built, the same engine also applies to AIS ships, trains, and seismic station self-checks. The tool would then be a general platform for receiver-from-catalogue localisation, with aircraft as the first catalogue.
